Book of NEAR: from zero to veteran

Every topic a learner needs, from "what is a private key" to stateless validation and Shade Agents. Each one is scored for difficulty, importance and how innovative NEAR's approach is, with an honest note on how it compares to Ethereum, Solana, Cosmos and others. Then a staged learning order, and a feasibility check on minting an NFT badge for each finished stage.

Part 1

Learning order: eight stages, eight badges

Stages go from universal crypto basics to NEAR-specific depth. Each ends with one real on-chain action and a badge mint. The opening quiz can skip people ahead, so a veteran or developer doesn't sit through Stage 0.

How the opening quiz routes people

Newcomer: Stages 0 → 1 → 2 → 4 → 5 → 6 → 7. Stage 3 (builder) is optional and unlocks the developer badge.
Crypto veteran: a test-out quiz awards Stage 0 and 1, then 2 → 4 → 5 → 6 → 7. The value for them is sharding, intents and chain signatures.
Developer: test out of 0, then 1 → 3 → 2 → 4 → 5 → 7. Stage 3 includes deploying a tiny contract on testnet.

Part 3

Showpiece plan: what to build first

Only lessons with something to do get a full page: a live read from the chain, a real transaction, or an animation you can drive. Each stage has two or three of them. Every other topic stays in the book as a backlog item, starting as a short text card with a quiz and growing into a full lesson if time allows.

Updated 30 Oct: the agreed MVP scope has changed from this showpiece list. Stage 5 is deferred, Stage 6 is dropped, and every card in Stages 1, 3 and 4 is in. The current scope and build plan are in the Book of NEAR Implementation Plan.

Transactions run on testnet so learners spend nothing, using a practice account the app funds from a faucet. The only mainnet actions are the badge mint and the optional tiny swap. Tier A is a stretch for 14 days solo, which is why the stage bar and lesson template should be built once and reused.

Part 4

NFT badges per stage: is it feasible?

Yes for minting and explorer visibility. "Official" status is a pitch, not a deliverable.

A NEP-171 contract that mints one badge per stage is a small, well-trodden build (about a day). Because NearBlocks and wallets index any contract that follows NEP-171 and emits standard NEP-297 events, the badges show up under the learner's account on NearBlocks and in NFT-aware wallets with no partnership needed. What does not exist today is a special "badge" or achievement slot on an explorer address page. Getting one means pitching NearBlocks or Pikespeak, and that won't be settled by 14 Nov. Present it as roadmap.

Which standard

  • NEP-171 + NEP-177 + NEP-297 (core, metadata, events): the standard everything indexes. Badges become visible in explorers and wallets automatically. Recommended.
  • NEP-393 (Soulbound Token): a NEAR standard built for credentials, with issuers, classes, revocation and "soul transfer" for account recovery. Used by the I-Am-Human registry (registry.i-am-human.near). Tooling and explorer support are thin, and SBTs use their own events, so they likely won't appear as NFTs on NearBlocks. verify NEP status and explorer support
  • Practical middle ground: a NEP-171 contract whose nft_transfer and nft_transfer_call panic. Displays everywhere like an NFT, but cannot move. You can add an owner-only "re-issue" call for lost accounts.

Soulbound or transferable?

You asked for badges people could exchange. The catch is that a tradeable credential can be bought, so it no longer proves anyone learned anything. Recommended split:

  • Stage badges: non-transferable. They are the proof of learning that an explorer or another app could trust.
  • "Book of NEAR" completion: a transferable collector edition minted alongside a non-transferable completion credential. People can trade or show off the collectible, while the credential stays with the learner.
  • "Used somewhere later": other apps can check nft_tokens_for_owner on your contract to gate perks (Discord roles, allowlists, discounts). That works only if the badge is non-transferable.

Cost per badge (mainnet estimate)

Storage staking (1 NEAR locks 100 KB). A badge with owner, title, description and media link plus hash is roughly 500–800 bytes≈ 0.005–0.008 Ⓝ
Gas for one mint call (about 10–20 Tgas at the minimum gas price)≈ 0.001–0.002 Ⓝ
Total per badge≈ 0.01 Ⓝ
1,000 learners × 8 badges, if you sponsor everything≈ 80 Ⓝ

Estimates from the documented storage price and default gas price; measure on testnet before committing. Keep images off-chain (IPFS or your Cloudflare site, with media_hash). Inlining an SVG on-chain would multiply storage cost.

Who pays

  • Learner attaches ~0.01 Ⓝ: simplest, but a true newcomer has no NEAR yet.
  • Sponsored: the contract pays storage from its own balance, and a relayer (NEP-366 meta-transactions) pays gas, so the mint is free for the learner. Cap it at one badge per account per stage and set a total budget. A hackathon budget of 10–20 Ⓝ covers 1,000–2,000 badges.
  • Recommended: sponsor Stages 0–1 so newcomers get a first badge free, and have later stages ask the learner to sign and pay, which doubles as their first real transaction.

How it shows up in explorers

  • NearBlocks: indexes fungible tokens, NFTs and multi-tokens. A compliant contract's mints appear in the account's NFT token transactions and token holdings, and the contract gets an NFT token page. The exact layout is from memory, so check it on testnet with a dummy mint. verify layout
  • Pikespeak: account analytics including NFT holdings for standard contracts. verify
  • Wallets (Meteor, MyNearWallet, HOT): NFT galleries read NEP-171 and NEP-177 metadata, so the badge art appears there too.
  • A dedicated "badge" on the address, like a verified tick or achievements row, does not exist today. That needs the explorer team to build it.

Path to "official" badges

  1. Ship a clean contract: NEP-171, 177 and 297, a clear issuer account, one public read method such as has_completed(account, stage), and an open-source repo.
  2. Give it a recognisable account name (for example book.yourname.near), collection metadata and an icon, so it looks legitimate in explorers.
  3. Demo it: in the X post, open your own address on NearBlocks and show the eight badges.
  4. After the hackathon, post a proposal on the NEAR governance forum (gov.near.org) and contact NearBlocks and Pikespeak with an "education achievements" spec. Optionally register as an NEP-393 issuer.
  5. The pitch to NEAR Foundation or DevHub: an ecosystem-wide credential for "understands NEAR", useful for grants, ambassador programs and hiring.

Risks and hackathon fit

Gaming and sybil farmingQuiz answers in the browser are trivially scripted. Mitigation: a backend checks quiz results and the learner's real testnet transactions, then signs a claim (account, stage, nonce). The contract checks the signature with env::ed25519_verify, allows one mint per account per stage, and requires stage N−1 first. Non-transferable badges remove the resale motive.
CostBounded and small: about 0.01 Ⓝ per badge. Put a budget cap and a per-account limit in the contract so a bot can't drain the sponsor balance.
Mainnet or testnetDo the lessons' practice transactions on testnet. Mint badges on mainnet: it's cheap, it's what judges can click on NearBlocks, and it counts as a real on-chain action. Deploy to testnet first during development.
Hackathon rulesThe contract must be written after 1 Nov. Today's work is design only. The badge contract plus relayer adds roughly 1–2 days of scope, which is fine within a 14-day window if the modules are kept to 5–8.
Judging upsideIt turns an explainer into a product with real mainnet usage, a measurable metric (badges minted), and a clean demo moment. It also fits the "real on-chain actions" requirement you set earlier.
Claims to keep honestDon't call the badges "official" or use NEAR branding in the art without permission. Say "proposed for explorer integration".
