# Lesson 3.2: Issue your own token

Issue: #20. Target time: 5 minutes. Chain access: testnet (practice account) or demo mode (DemoChain). Views: 2D and 3D, one shared state.

Topic: `3.2-topic.md` (the source of truth for what this lesson teaches).

## 1. Learning goals

1. Issue a fungible token following the NEP-141 standard.
2. Understand why token transfers require a one-time storage registration (NEP-145) to prevent state attacks.
3. Send a token transfer gaslessly using a native meta-transaction (NEP-366) paid by a relayer.

## 2. Steps

### Step 1: Hook (Minting your own currency)

- **Caption:** Create your own fungible token in seconds. On NEAR, tokens follow the NEP-141 standard.
- **Explain:** Just like ERC-20 on Ethereum, NEAR has **NEP-141**. You specify a name, ticker symbol (e.g. `BOOK`), and total supply. The tokens are minted directly into your account's balance.
- **Scene:** Your account mailbox with a golden printing stamp and an empty coin stack waiting to be minted. No gate.

### Step 2: Mint the tokens

- **Caption:** Mint 1,000 {symbol} tokens into your account.
- **Explain:** The token contract creates the balance entry and locks the initial storage deposit for your account. You now hold the entire initial circulating supply.
- **Action:** Field: Symbol (default `BOOK`), Supply (default `1000`). Button **Mint {symbol} tokens**. A stack of minted coins appears beside your mailbox. Gate opens when minted.
- **Evidence:** `{ tokenSymbol: "{symbol}", totalSupply: 1000 }`.

### Step 3: The storage trap (Why the transfer fails)

- **Caption:** Try to send 50 {symbol} to a friend's mailbox. Watch the chain refuse.
- **Explain:** On Ethereum, anyone can airdrop dust tokens to a million accounts. On NEAR, every balance entry requires storage staking. If the receiver is not registered, the transfer fails: `The account is not registered to hold this token`.
- **Action:** Button **Send 50 {symbol} to friend.testnet**. The parcel stops at the friend's mailbox with a red padlock and an error message: `Account unregistered (NEP-145)`. Gate opens after seeing the refusal.

### Step 4: Storage registration (NEP-145)

- **Caption:** Pay a tiny storage deposit (~0.00125 NEAR) to register your friend's mailbox.
- **Explain:** Under **NEP-145**, an account registers with a token contract by depositing a fraction of a cent in NEAR. This permanently reserves the bytes needed to store their token balance.
- **Action:** Button **Pay storage deposit for friend**. A tiny droplet of NEAR unlocks the padlock on friend.testnet's mailbox. Gate opens when registered.

### Step 5: Gasless transfer via relayer (NEP-366)

- **Caption:** Send the 50 {symbol} tokens without spending any NEAR. A relayer covers your gas.
- **Explain:** What if a user has tokens but zero native NEAR? Under **NEP-366**, you sign an off-chain payload (`SignedDelegateAction`). A **Relayer** submits it to the network and pays the gas in NEAR on your behalf.
- **Action:** Button **Sign gasless transfer**. A relayer courier picks up the signed parcel, pays the gas fee, and delivers 50 {symbol} coins to your friend's mailbox while your NEAR balance stays untouched. Gate opens on delivery. The demo relayer pays for up to 3 transfers per visit, then refuses; it counts in memory, so a reload resets it. The signed delegate action stays valid for 200 blocks (about 2 to 4 minutes).
- **Evidence:** `{ relayerPaidGas: true, txHash }`.

### Step 6: Quick check (summary page)

- **Caption:** Three questions on NEP-141 tokens, storage deposits, and relayers.
- **Action:** Check (section 3).

### Step 7: Honest take

- **Caption:** Honest take: Storage registration friction vs. native protocol meta-transactions.
- **Gate:** Gated until the gasless transfer completes in step 5.

## 3. Check

**Q1. Why does sending a new token to an unregistered account fail by default?**
- A. The recipient has not deposited storage staking to cover their balance record in the contract. **(correct)**
- B. The blockchain requires a government passport to receive tokens. *Wrong: NEAR is permissionless.*
- C. The token contract has run out of internet bandwidth. *Wrong: blockchain storage is economic, not a bandwidth issue.*

**Q2. How do native meta-transactions (NEP-366) benefit new users?**
- A. Users can interact and transfer tokens without needing native NEAR tokens for gas upfront. **(correct)**
- B. They make all transactions permanently untraceable. *Wrong: meta-transactions are public on-chain.*
- C. They eliminate the need for private keys entirely. *Wrong: users still sign the action off-chain.*

**Q3. What happens to the storage deposit if an account sells all its tokens and unregisters?**
- A. The storage deposit is fully refunded to the user. **(correct)**
- B. It is transferred to the token contract owner as profit. *Wrong: storage deposits belong to the account holder.*
- C. It is converted into lottery tickets. *Wrong: blockchain deposits follow strict cryptographic rules.*

## 4. Honest take

**Strengths:**
- **Protection from spam storage bloat:** Contract creators cannot be bankrupted by malicious users generating millions of dust balance entries. [near-storage-mgmt]
- **Protocol-level meta-transactions:** Gasless onboarding works natively without deploying complex ERC-4337 smart contract wallets. [near-meta-tx]
- **Refundable registration:** Empty accounts can unregister to reclaim their locked storage deposits. [near-storage-mgmt]

**Weaknesses:**
- **Onboarding friction:** First-time recipients often encounter failed transfers if the sender forgets to bundle the recipient's `storage_deposit`. [near-storage-mgmt]
- **Dual-action requirement:** Apps must routinely execute compound transactions that attach both NEAR storage deposits and token transfers. [near-storage-mgmt]

Compared with: **Ethereum ERC-20** (no storage registration, but gasless transfers require complex ERC-4337 bundlers and paymasters).
