# Lesson 3.1: Deploy a contract in one click

Issue: #19. Target time: 5 minutes. Chain access: testnet (practice account) or demo mode (DemoChain). Views: 2D and 3D, one shared state.

Topic: `3.1-topic.md` (the source of truth for what this lesson teaches).

## 1. Learning goals

1. Deploy a pre-compiled WebAssembly (`.wasm`) smart contract to a new sub-account.
2. Understand NEAR's storage staking model: locking 1 NEAR per 100 KB instead of paying ongoing rent.
3. Observe how locked storage deposits are refunded to available balance when state is deleted.

## 2. Steps

### Step 1: Hook (Code that lives on a name)

- **Caption:** Where do smart contracts live on NEAR? Right on an account name, running standard WebAssembly.
- **Explain:** On Ethereum, deploying a contract gives you a random hex address (`0x8f...`). On NEAR, smart contracts live directly on accounts or **sub-accounts** that you control (like `counter.{name}.testnet`), compiled to standard **WebAssembly** (Wasm).
- **Scene:** Your learner mailbox with a small dashed sub-mailbox attached to its post labeled `counter.{name}.testnet`. A rolled-up scroll with a gear symbol sits ready. No gate.

### Step 2: The sub-account and the Wasm bytecode

- **Caption:** Create a sub-account and inspect the WebAssembly binary (~10 KB of bytecode).
- **Explain:** Any account can create sub-accounts without asking a registrar. In a single batch transaction, you create `counter.{name}.testnet`, fund it, and deploy the counter Wasm code.
- **Scene:** The sub-mailbox solidifies. The scroll unrolls slightly, revealing WebAssembly binary instructions. Next is open.

### Step 3: Deploy and lock storage deposit

- **Caption:** Deploy the contract. Watch {storageDeposit} NEAR lock into storage staking.
- **Explain:** NEAR does not charge monthly hosting rent. Instead, you lock **1 NEAR per 100 KB** of data stored. For a 10 KB contract, about 0.1 NEAR moves from your **available balance** to your **locked storage deposit**.
- **Action:** Button **Deploy counter contract**. The scroll with the gear mounts atop the sub-mailbox. The balance gauge splits into available liquid NEAR and locked storage NEAR. Gate opens when deployed.
- **Evidence:** `{ contractId: "counter.{name}.testnet", txHash }`.

### Step 4: Call contract methods and expand state

- **Caption:** Click Increment. The contract updates its internal counter and saves the value.
- **Explain:** When you call `increment`, the WebAssembly runtime executes the method. A new key-value pair is written to the account's state trie. Notice the tiny additional byte storage allocated.
- **Action:** Button **Increment** (updates count to 1, 2, 3...). The counter display on the scroll advances. Gate opens after at least one increment.

### Step 5: Delete state and reclaim your NEAR

- **Caption:** Clean up data. When you delete records, your locked NEAR is instantly refunded.
- **Explain:** On Ethereum, deleting state rarely refunds significant gas. On NEAR, storage staking is a refundable security deposit. Clearing state immediately returns the locked NEAR to your spendable balance.
- **Action:** Button **Reset & clear state**. The counter drops to zero, state records are removed, and the storage gauge moves back into your available liquid balance. Gate opens when refunded.

### Step 6: Quick check (summary page)

- **Caption:** Three questions on WebAssembly, sub-accounts, and storage staking.
- **Action:** Check (section 3).

### Step 7: Honest take

- **Caption:** Honest take: Storage staking vs. Ethereum state bloat.
- **Gate:** Gated until the learner deployed the contract in step 3.

## 3. Check

**Q1. How is data storage paid for on NEAR?**
- A. By locking a refundable deposit of 1 NEAR per 100 KB of data stored. **(correct)**
- B. By paying a monthly subscription fee with a credit card. *Wrong: smart contracts are governed by on-chain tokens.*
- C. Storage is completely free and unlimited for everyone. *Wrong: unmetered storage causes catastrophic state bloat.*

**Q2. What happens to your locked storage NEAR when you delete stored data?**
- A. It is unlocked and returned to your available spendable balance. **(correct)**
- B. It is burned and lost forever. *Wrong: storage deposits are refundable.*
- C. It is sent to the validator as a bonus. *Wrong: validators do not keep user storage deposits.*

**Q3. What code format do NEAR smart contracts compile down to?**
- A. Standard WebAssembly (Wasm). **(correct)**
- B. Solidity bytecode (EVM only). *Wrong: NEAR's native engine is Wasm.*
- C. Raw Python scripts. *Wrong: contracts compile to high-performance Wasm binaries.*

## 4. Honest take

**Strengths:**
- **Sustainable state economics:** Prevents the blockchain state bloat crisis by making storage economically self-cleaning through refundable deposits. [near-storage]
- **Language flexibility:** Developers can write in Rust or JavaScript; both compile to standard Wasm. [near-contracts]
- **Intuitive sub-accounts:** Hierarchical naming (`app.user.testnet`) organizes contract deployments cleanly. [near-account-id]

**Weaknesses:**
- **Upfront deposit barrier:** Deploying a large contract (e.g. 200 KB) requires locking 2 NEAR upfront, which can surprise new builders. [near-storage]
- **`LackBalanceForState` errors:** If an account runs out of available balance to cover storage for incoming data, transactions revert until topped up. [near-storage]

Compared with: **Ethereum** (one-time `SSTORE` gas fee, state stored forever for free, zero incentive to clean up state).
