# Lesson 4.2: Swap across chains with an intent

Issue: #24. Target time: 5 minutes. Chain access: live 1Click dry-run quote (or demo mode with recorded quote). Optional tiny mainnet swap if funded. Views: 2D and 3D, one shared state.

Topic: `4.2-topic.md` (the source of truth for what this lesson teaches).

## 1. Learning goals

1. Understand the difference between an imperative transaction and a declarative intent.
2. Request a live cross-chain swap quote through the 1Click API without paying fees.
3. Observe how decentralized solvers compete to deliver native assets in seconds.

## 2. Steps

### Step 1: Hook (Stop bridging, start expressing intents)

- **Caption:** Why spend 20 minutes bridging tokens when you can just declare what you want?
- **Explain:** With traditional bridges, you must approve tokens, wrap them, bridge them, pay two different gas currencies, and wait. With **NEAR Intents**, you simply sign what you want (e.g., "Trade 5 NEAR for USDC on Arbitrum").
- **Scene:** Two islands representing different blockchains (NEAR and Arbitrum). In between sits the decentralized Solver Network. No gate.

### Step 2: Declare your intent

- **Caption:** Choose your trade pair: Swap 5 NEAR for native USDC on Arbitrum.
- **Explain:** You specify only the input asset and desired output asset. You do not specify which bridge or DEX to use. Competing third-party market makers (**solvers**) will figure out the most optimal route.
- **Action:** Select origin asset (NEAR) and destination asset (USDC on Arbitrum). Amount: 5 NEAR. Button **Fetch live intent quote**. Next unlocks when the quote arrives.

### Step 3: Inspect the 1Click quote

- **Caption:** Inspect the winning solver quote: {usdcOutput} USDC delivered in under {timeEstimate} seconds.
- **Explain:** The **1Click API** aggregates bids across solvers. The winning quote provides a guaranteed minimum output (`minAmountOut`), protecting you from slippage and front-running.
- **Scene:** An intent parcel appears, showing the guaranteed exchange rate, zero destination gas fees, and a cryptographic solver signature.
- **Action:** Button **Inspect quote breakdown**. Review solver delivery time (~27 seconds) and fee details. Gate opens immediately.
- **Evidence:** `{ originAsset: "NEAR", destinationAsset: "USDC", estimatedSeconds: 27 }`.

### Step 4: The solver fulfillment simulation

- **Caption:** Watch how the solver fulfills the order: instant liquidity on Arbitrum, zero bridge delay.
- **Explain:** The solver doesn't wait for a bridge. They already maintain deep liquidity on Arbitrum. They deposit native USDC directly to your Arbitrum address, then claim your NEAR deposit on the NEAR verifier contract (`intents.near`).
- **Action:** Button **Simulate solver execution**. The solver courier delivers native USDC coins directly to your destination mailbox on Arbitrum. Gate opens on delivery.

### Step 5: Optional live swap or dry-run completion

- **Caption:** You can test with live mainnet funds if connected, or complete safely on the dry-run quote.
- **Explain:** For learners with a connected, funded mainnet account, the app offers an optional tiny real swap capped at $1. Otherwise, the lesson completes on the verified dry-run quote with zero money spent.
- **Action:** Choice: "Finish on verified quote" (default) or "Execute tiny mainnet swap". Gate opens on selection.
- **Evidence:** `{ realSwapExecuted: false, dryQuoteVerified: true }`.

## 3. Check

**Q1. What is the fundamental difference between an intent and a traditional transaction?**
- A. An intent specifies the desired outcome; third-party solvers find and execute the optimal path. **(correct)**
- B. An intent is only written on paper with a pen. *Wrong: intents are cryptographically signed digital declarations.*
- C. An intent requires you to pay with credit card points. *Wrong: intents use standard crypto assets.*

**Q2. How do solvers deliver funds to the destination chain so quickly?**
- A. They front native liquidity from their own pre-existing capital pools on the destination chain. **(correct)**
- B. They make the destination blockchain run 10,000 times faster. *Wrong: blockchain block times are fixed.*
- C. They cancel all other transactions on the network. *Wrong: solvers operate within standard consensus.*

**Q3. Do you need destination gas tokens (like ETH on Arbitrum) to receive funds from an intent swap?**
- A. No, solvers handle all destination gas requirements as part of the bundled quote. **(correct)**
- B. Yes, you must always own at least 1 ETH before swapping. *Wrong: solvers abstract away gas completely.*
- C. Yes, you must send physical cash to the miner. *Wrong: all fees are settled automatically on-chain.*

## 4. Honest take

**Strengths:**
- **Zero gas headaches:** Trade between disparate chains without acquiring foreign gas tokens. [near-intents]
- **MEV & sandwich attack immunity:** Orders are private until matched off-chain, protecting retail traders from front-running. [near-intents]
- **Guaranteed execution price:** Quoted minimum outputs are cryptographically locked; no surprise slippage. [spike-6]

**Weaknesses:**
- **Market maker dependency:** Exotic or low-liquidity token pairs depend on active solver participation; without solvers, quotes are unavailable. [near-intents]
- **Mainnet capital focus:** Intent networks run primarily on mainnet with real capital, making testnet integration sparse. [spike-6]

Compared with: **Uniswap / Traditional Bridges** (slow multi-step bridging, high slippage, and heavy MEV extraction).
