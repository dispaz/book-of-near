# Lesson 4.1: One account, every chain

Issue: #22. Target time: 6 minutes. Chain access: testnet (practice account calls `v1.signer-prod.testnet`) or demo mode. Views: 2D and 3D, one shared state. Showpiece, 3D first.

Topic: `4.1-topic.md` (the source of truth for what this lesson teaches).

## 1. Learning goals

1. Derive an authentic Ethereum address deterministically from a NEAR account name using a derivation path.
2. Request a decentralized Multi-Party Computation (MPC) signature from `v1.signer-prod.testnet`.
3. Attach the signature to an Ethereum transaction and cryptographically verify that the recovered signer matches the derived address.

## 2. Steps

### Step 1: Hook (The wallet nightmare)

- **Caption:** Ethereum needs its own wallet and its own private key. Could one NEAR account control the Ethereum address instead?
- **Explain:** Normally, controlling an Ethereum address means holding its private key, so each chain needs its own wallet and seed phrase. On NEAR, an account can get signatures for an Ethereum address through decentralized **Chain Signatures**, with no Ethereum key stored anywhere.
- **Scene:** The learner's NEAR mailbox in the foreground. In the background stands a ring of MPC validator nodes around an Ethereum ledger. No gate.

### Step 2: Deterministic address derivation

- **Caption:** Derive an Ethereum address from your NEAR name ({name}) and the path "ethereum-1".
- **Explain:** Your Ethereum address is computed mathematically from your NEAR Account ID, a chosen **derivation path**, and the MPC network's master public key. It requires zero network calls and zero fees; it calculates in milliseconds.
- **Action:** Field: Derivation path (default `"ethereum-1"`). Button **Derive Ethereum address**. A mathematical light beam projects from your mailbox through the MPC ring onto the Ethereum ledger, carving the address `{derivedEthAddress}`. Gate opens on derivation.
- **Evidence:** `{ derivedEthAddress, path: "ethereum-1" }`.

### Step 3: Prepare the Ethereum transaction

- **Caption:** Construct a native Sepolia transfer. Notice that your new Ethereum address has 0 ETH.
- **Explain:** The transaction is standard Ethereum (EVM) data: recipient, value, gas limit, nonce, and chain ID. Because the address is fresh, it has no ETH to pay gas, so it cannot broadcast on its own yet. The app hashes the transaction into a 32-byte payload, which is the only thing the MPC network signs.
- **Action:** Button **Prepare payload**. The transaction fields (to, value, gas, nonce) assemble into a stamped parcel, and the app shows its 32-byte hash `{payloadHash}`. Gate opens immediately.

### Step 4: Request the MPC threshold signature

- **Caption:** Ask the MPC validator network (`v1.signer-prod.testnet`) to sign the Ethereum payload hash.
- **Explain:** You call `sign()` on the signer contract with the payload, the derivation path, and the key type (ECDSA on secp256k1, the curve Ethereum uses). You attach **1 yoctoNEAR** and gas. The validators each compute a key share of the signature. No single machine sees the private key.
- **Action:** Button **Request signature (MPC)**. A countdown runs while the validators stamp their key shares (from about 10 s on testnet, 5.5 s in demo mode). When it returns, three values appear in the parcel: `r` (32 bytes), `s` (32 bytes), and `v` (the recovery id, 0 or 1). Gate opens when the signature returns.
- **Evidence:** `{ mpcTxHash, signatureReturned: true, r, s, v }`.

### Step 5: Attach the signature and verify the signer

- **Caption:** Attach r, s and v to the transaction, then recover the signer address with viem. If the address matches, your NEAR account signed for Ethereum.
- **Explain:** An Ethereum signature is the pair `r` and `s` plus the recovery id `v`. Ethereum software uses them to compute the signer's public key, then takes the last 20 bytes of that key's hash (Keccak-256, the hash function Ethereum uses). That is the address. Here, `ecrecover` runs on the same payload hash with `r`, `s` and `v`. If the result equals the derived address, the MPC network signed for that address, and no one holds an Ethereum private key.
- **Action:** Button **Verify signer**. The app shows the signed transaction's fields (`r`, `s`, `v`), then the recovered address next to `{derivedEthAddress}`. The verification badge illuminates green: `Recovered address matches derived address`. Next is open.

### Step 6: Quick check (summary page)

- **Caption:** Three questions on Chain Signatures, MPC nodes, and address derivation.
- **Action:** Check (section 3).

### Step 7: Honest take

- **Caption:** Honest take: Chain Signatures vs. cross-chain bridges and smart contract wallets.
- **Gate:** Gated until the verification in step 5 passes.

## 3. Check

**Q1. Who holds the private key for the derived Ethereum address?**
- A. Nobody holds the complete private key; it exists only as distributed mathematical shares across MPC nodes. **(correct)**
- B. The NEAR Foundation CEO holds it on a flash drive. *Wrong: the protocol is decentralized.*
- C. Google Cloud holds it in an encrypted spreadsheet. *Wrong: cryptography is handled by on-chain MPC validators.*

**Q2. How much does it cost to derive a new foreign address from your NEAR account?**
- A. Zero NEAR; derivation is calculated offline via pure mathematics. **(correct)**
- B. Exactly 100 NEAR per address. *Wrong: derivation requires zero on-chain fees.*
- C. A $50 monthly subscription. *Wrong: crypto derivation is open-source and free.*

**Q3. If a derived Ethereum address has 0 ETH, can it broadcast a transaction without external help?**
- A. No, Ethereum requires native gas tokens (or a relayer/paymaster) to pay mining/validation fees. **(correct)**
- B. Yes, Ethereum transactions never require gas. *Wrong: all Ethereum transactions consume gas.*
- C. Yes, NEAR pays the Ethereum validators on its behalf. *Wrong: the address must hold ETH or use a relayer to pay gas.*

## 4. Honest take

**Strengths:**
- **Multichain sovereignty without contracts:** One NEAR account controls an Ethereum address without deploying a wallet contract on Ethereum. [near-chain-sigs]
- **Smart contract control:** A NEAR DAO or smart contract can programmatically control an Ethereum treasury. [near-chain-sigs]
- **Zero-bridge native assets:** The signed transaction executes directly on Ethereum; no wrapped token honeypots. [near-chain-sigs]

**Weaknesses:**
- **MPC signing latency:** The signing nodes need several seconds to agree on a signature (7 to 14 seconds on testnet in our runs, from sending the request to a final answer), slower than signing with a local private key. [spike-5]
- **Foreign gas bootstrapping:** A newly derived address requires ETH (or a gas relayer) before its signed transactions can broadcast. [spike-5]
- **Validator threshold trust:** Security relies on the cryptographic assumption that a threshold majority of MPC validators remain honest. [near-chain-sigs]

Compared with: **Cross-chain Bridges** (vulnerable to smart contract hacks) and **Account Abstraction (ERC-4337)** (EVM-only; needs a contract wallet deployed on each chain).

## 6. What the built lesson adds (code follows the script; these are extras on top)

- **Try another path (step 2).** After deriving, the learner can press **Try another path**, type a new path (for example `eth/defi`) and derive again. A new path gives a new address, and the captions use it.
- **Countdown (step 4).** While the request waits, the status reads "Waiting for the signing network… about N s left" (from about 5.5 s in demo mode, about 10 s on testnet), then "any moment now" if it runs over.
- **Threshold display (step 4).** The figure draws 8 signing nodes. Their key shares light up as the request waits. When the signature returns, 6 of the 8 (the threshold) are lit, not all of them. The numbers are an illustration; the real network's size differs.
- **"Not sent to Sepolia" note (step 5).** After the check, a note says the transaction was not broadcast: the derived address starts with 0 ETH, and the signed bytes could be broadcast once the address is funded, by a faucet or a gas relayer.

### Scene notes (as built, both views, one state)

- **The page is split in two.** The NEAR side (left): the learner's mailbox, the signer contract (a scroll) and the ring of 8 signing nodes round it. The Ethereum side (right): an area marked on the paper (`ChainArea`): a light tint of the stage accent, a dashed ink border, "Ethereum (Sepolia)" printed at its front edge and a short chain of blocks along its back edge. It rises from the page with the rest of the figure. Picking the area (step 1) explains Ethereum.
- **Step 2.** The address is a dashed mailbox inside the area until it is derived; then it grows up from the page there. The beam runs from the NEAR mailbox, through the ring's middle, into the area to the address.
- **Step 3.** The transaction (a parcel) is packed on the NEAR side, by the learner's mailbox.
- **Step 4, the signing animation**, driven by the real wait (`signAnim` in `signing.ts`): the parcel, carrying the payload hash, travels to the signer contract (`travel`, while the request is sent); the scroll's gear turns, a pulse spreads round the ring with lines out to every node (the nodes pick up the request), the nodes that have not answered yet work (a small staggered turn), and each node's share chip flies to a meeting spot inside the ring, between the nodes and off the signer contract (`SHARE_MEET`), as the countdown runs (`work`; chips = lit nodes, so they keep pace with the countdown and stop short of the threshold however long the wait). The nodes combine their shares off chain: the contract never sees a share, only the finished signature. When the answer comes, the last chips fly in up to 6 of 8 (`shares`, 0.8 s), a short flash at the meeting spot joins them and only the finished r, s and v slips drop from there onto the parcel (`sealed`, 0.7 s), then the parcel comes home with them (`done`). 2D draws a lighter version: dashed pulses along the lines, share dots flying to the meeting spot, nodes filling, a flash there, the slips dropping onto the parcel. The step-4 facts say in one line that 8 nodes and a threshold of 6 are a drawing. Reduced motion: no motion and no finale; each phase shows its end state, and the status text still updates.
- **Step 5.** After the page turns, the stamped parcel, carrying the r, s and v slips, moves into the Ethereum area beside the address. After **Verify signer**, the address's flag goes up and the check sits by it ("= signer ✓" in its name in 3D; "recovered signer = this address ✓" under it in 2D), and the "Not sent to Sepolia" note is printed in the area.
