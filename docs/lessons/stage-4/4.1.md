# Lesson 4.1: One account, every chain

Issue: #22. Target time: 6 minutes. Chain access: testnet (practice account calls `v1.signer-prod.testnet`) or demo mode. Views: 2D and 3D, one shared state. Showpiece, 3D first.

Topic: `4.1-topic.md` (the source of truth for what this lesson teaches).

## 1. Learning goals

1. Derive an authentic Ethereum address deterministically from a NEAR account name using a derivation path.
2. Request a decentralized Multi-Party Computation (MPC) signature from `v1.signer-prod.testnet`.
3. Cryptographically verify that the recovered signer of the foreign transaction matches the derived address.

## 2. Steps

### Step 1: Hook (The multi-wallet nightmare)

- **Caption:** Ethereum, Bitcoin, Solana, Cosmos. Why do you need four different wallets and four different seed phrases?
- **Explain:** Blockchain fragmentation forces users to download separate wallet extensions and manage multiple private keys. On NEAR, a single account can sign native transactions on any blockchain using decentralized **Chain Signatures**.
- **Scene:** The learner's NEAR mailbox in the foreground. In the background stands a ring of MPC validator nodes surrounding foreign blockchain ledgers (Ethereum, Bitcoin, Solana). No gate.

### Step 2: Deterministic address derivation

- **Caption:** Derive an Ethereum address from your NEAR name ({name}) and the path "ethereum-1".
- **Explain:** Your foreign address is computed mathematically from your NEAR Account ID, a chosen **derivation path**, and the MPC network's master public key. It requires zero network calls and zero fees—it calculates in milliseconds.
- **Action:** Field: Derivation path (default `"ethereum-1"`). Button **Derive Ethereum address**. A mathematical light beam projects from your mailbox through the MPC ring onto the Ethereum ledger, carving the address `{derivedEthAddress}`. Gate opens on derivation.
- **Evidence:** `{ derivedEthAddress, path: "ethereum-1" }`.

### Step 3: Prepare the foreign transaction

- **Caption:** Construct a native Sepolia transfer. Notice that your new Ethereum address has 0 ETH.
- **Explain:** The Ethereum transaction is standard EVM bytecode. Because it is a fresh address, it starts with zero gas balance. The app prepares the 32-byte payload hash for the MPC network to sign.
- **Action:** Button **Prepare payload**. The raw Ethereum transaction fields (to, value, gas, nonce) assemble into a stamped parcel ready for signature. Gate opens immediately.

### Step 4: Request the MPC threshold signature

- **Caption:** Ask the MPC validator network (`v1.signer-prod.testnet`) to sign the Ethereum payload.
- **Explain:** You call `sign()` on the signer contract, attaching **1 yoctoNEAR** deposit and gas. The decentralized validator nodes calculate threshold key shares without any single machine ever seeing the private key.
- **Action:** Button **Request signature (MPC)**. A 5-second countdown timer runs as the MPC validator figures stamp their key shares. Gate opens when the threshold signature ($r, s, v$) returns.
- **Evidence:** `{ mpcTxHash, signatureReturned: true }`.

### Step 5: Cryptographic verification

- **Caption:** Recover the signer address using viem. The math proves your NEAR account signed for Ethereum!
- **Explain:** Using standard Ethereum cryptography (`ecrecover`), we verify that the signature produced by the MPC nodes mathematically recovers to `{derivedEthAddress}`. You control this Ethereum address completely from NEAR.
- **Action:** Button **Verify signer**. The verification badge illuminates green: `Recovered address matches derived address`. Next is open.

### Step 6: Quick check (summary page)

- **Caption:** Three questions on Chain Signatures, MPC nodes, and address derivation.
- **Action:** Check (section 3).

### Step 7: Honest take

- **Caption:** Honest take: Chain Signatures vs. cross-chain bridges and smart contract wallets.
- **Gate:** Gated until the verification in step 5 passes.

## 3. Check

**Q1. Who holds the private key for the derived Ethereum address?**
- A. Nobody holds the complete private key; it exists only as distributed mathematical shares across MPC nodes. **(correct)**
- B. The NEAR Foundation CEO holds it on a flash drive. *Wrong: the protocol is decentralized.*
- C. Google Cloud holds it in an encrypted spreadsheet. *Wrong: cryptography is handled by on-chain MPC validators.*

**Q2. How much does it cost to derive a new foreign address from your NEAR account?**
- A. Zero NEAR; derivation is calculated offline via pure mathematics. **(correct)**
- B. Exactly 100 NEAR per address. *Wrong: derivation requires zero on-chain fees.*
- C. A $50 monthly subscription. *Wrong: crypto derivation is open-source and free.*

**Q3. If a derived Ethereum address has 0 ETH, can it broadcast a transaction without external help?**
- A. No, Ethereum requires native gas tokens (or a relayer/paymaster) to pay mining/validation fees. **(correct)**
- B. Yes, Ethereum transactions never require gas. *Wrong: all Ethereum transactions consume gas.*
- C. Yes, NEAR pays Ethereum miners directly in Bitcoin. *Wrong: each blockchain requires its own native gas.*

## 4. Honest take

**Strengths:**
- **True multichain sovereignty:** One NEAR account controls addresses on Bitcoin, Ethereum, Solana, and Cosmos without deploying foreign contracts. [near-chain-sigs]
- **Smart contract control:** A NEAR DAO or smart contract can programmatically control foreign blockchain treasuries. [near-chain-sigs]
- **Zero-bridge native assets:** Transactions execute directly on target chains; no wrapped token honeypots. [near-chain-sigs]

**Weaknesses:**
- **MPC signing latency:** Threshold consensus requires 4 to 6 seconds to coordinate node shares, slower than local private key signing. [spike-5]
- **Foreign gas bootstrapping:** A newly derived address requires foreign gas funds (or an intent relayer) before its signed transactions can broadcast. [spike-5]
- **Validator threshold trust:** Security relies on the cryptographic assumption that a threshold majority of MPC validators remain honest. [near-chain-sigs]

Compared with: **Cross-chain Bridges** (vulnerable to smart contract hacks) and **Account Abstraction (ERC-4337)** (EVM-only, cannot sign for Bitcoin or Solana).
