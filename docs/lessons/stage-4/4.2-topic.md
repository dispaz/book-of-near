## In one sentence

NEAR Intents let users state what outcome they want (e.g. "swap 10 NEAR for USDC on Arbitrum") while competing third-party market makers (solvers) take on the cross-chain execution complexity and fulfill the order in seconds.

## The problem it solves

Traditional cross-chain trading is notoriously tedious and error-prone. A user wanting to move from NEAR to Arbitrum must find a bridge, acquire ETH for destination gas fees, wrap assets, wait 15–30 minutes for cross-chain message confirmations, and navigate decentralized exchange slippage. If any transaction along the chain fails, funds can become stranded. [near-intents]

## How it works on NEAR

**The Intent Paradigm: Outcome over Execution.**
- **Traditional Transaction:** The user specifies every single imperative step: `approve()`, `deposit()`, `bridge()`, `swap()`. If gas spikes or liquidity changes, the transaction reverts or suffers severe slippage. [near-intents]
- **Intent:** The user specifies only the desired end state: "I will provide $X$ of Token A; I require at least $Y$ of Token B delivered to my address before time $T$." [near-intents]

**The Solver Network (Defuse Protocol).**
- Third-party market makers known as **Solvers** listen to intent requests over a real-time message bus. [near-intents]
- Solvers compete against each other to offer the best price and fastest execution. [near-intents]
- Solvers front the liquidity on the destination chain immediately from their own balance sheets, eliminating bridge confirmation wait times for the end user. [near-intents]
- Settlement is cryptographically guaranteed and cleared on NEAR via the Verifier contract (`intents.near`). [near-intents]

**The 1Click API.**
- A high-level REST API and SDK (`https://1click.chaindefuser.com`) that abstracts intent construction and status tracking. [spike-6]
- `GET /v0/tokens`: Lists available assets across blockchains (NEAR, Ethereum, Arbitrum, Solana, Bitcoin). [spike-6]
- `POST /v0/quote` with `dry: true`: Generates a cryptographically signed quote without requiring a private key or deposit. [spike-6]
- **Quote shape:** Returns exact input, expected output, guaranteed minimum output (`minAmountOut`), estimated completion time (~20–30s), fees, and deadline. [spike-6]
- **Note on Solvers in API:** The 1Click API returns the single best winning quote aggregated across solvers; it does not stream raw losing bids. [spike-6]

## Words to know

- **Intent:** A signed declaration of a desired economic outcome rather than a specific transaction execution path.
- **Solver (Market Maker):** An off-chain actor that quotes, routes, and fulfills user intents using its own liquidity.
- **Defuse:** The underlying cross-chain intent protocol and settlement layer on NEAR.
- **1Click API:** The developer interface used to query tokens and execute dry or live intent quotes.
- **Slippage Tolerance:** The maximum percentage difference between quoted price and execution price accepted by the user.

## Common misunderstandings

- **"The user has to wait for slow cross-chain bridges."** No. Solvers provide instant liquidity on the destination chain using their own pre-existing capital pools, taking on the bridging delay themselves. [near-intents]
- **"Solvers can steal the user's deposit."** No. The verifier smart contract only releases the user's funds to the solver if cryptographic proof of destination delivery is verified before the deadline. [near-intents]
- **"You need destination gas tokens."** No. Solvers handle destination gas as part of the bundled quote; users only pay in the asset they are trading. [near-intents]

## Compared with Traditional Bridges and DEXs

- **Automated Market Makers (Uniswap):** Users trade against on-chain liquidity pools, exposing themselves to MEV (maximal extractable value), front-running, and high slippage. [eth-amm]
- **Bridges:** Users lock tokens and wait for multi-sig relays to mint wrapped tokens. [near-bridge-risk]
- **NEAR Intents:** Zero MEV exposure (orders are private until matched off-chain), guaranteed execution price, and near-instant settlement. [near-intents]

## Strengths and weaknesses (feeds the Honest take)

**Strengths:**
- Superior UX: Single-click swaps across completely different chains (e.g. Bitcoin to Arbitrum) without manual bridging or destination gas tokens. [near-intents]
- MEV protection: Intent auction happens off-chain, protecting retail users from sandwich attacks. [near-intents]
- Fast settlement: User receives destination funds in seconds rather than waiting for optimistic challenge windows. [near-intents]

**Weaknesses:**
- Liquidity dependence: Rare token pairs or extremely large trades depend on active solver participation and liquidity depth. [near-intents]
- Mainnet-first ecosystem: 1Click API runs on mainnet with real capital; testnet infrastructure for intents is limited, requiring recorded quotes for safe educational demos. [spike-6]
