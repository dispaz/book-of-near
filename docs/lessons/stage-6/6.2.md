# Lesson 6.2: The live money map

Issue: #87. Target time: 5 minutes. Chain access: none (recorded data in both modes). Views: 2D and 3D, one
shared state.

Facts come from `docs/lessons/stage-6/6.2-topic.md`. Every number is **recorded** (13 Nov 2026, from
DefiLlama and NEAR mainnet view calls) and the page says so: "Recorded on 13 Nov 2026. Not live data." The
lesson fetches nothing. The swap is worked out from recorded pool quotes and sends nothing.

## 1. Learning goals

1. Name the four building blocks of NEAR DeFi: stablecoins, an exchange (Rhea Dex), lending (Rhea Lend) and
   oracles.
2. Tell a native stablecoin (USDC, issued by Circle on NEAR) from a bridged copy (USDC.e).
3. See that an oracle's price has an age, and that a contract must refuse an old one.
4. See why a stable pool swaps same-value tokens near 1:1 while a simple pool's price moves with trade size.

## 2. Steps

### Step 1: The money map
- **Caption:** What turns a blockchain into an economy? Stable money, swaps, loans and prices.
- **Explain:** DeFi (decentralized finance) is money services run by smart contracts that anyone can use,
  with no bank in the middle. On NEAR the four corners are stablecoins, an exchange (Rhea Dex, which was
  Ref Finance), lending (Rhea Lend, which was Burrow) and oracles. Together they held about $162 million
  (TVL, total value locked) on 13 Nov 2026. Pick any corner.
- **Scene:** The four corners on the page: a USDC mailbox, an oracle booth, two Rhea pools and the Rhea Lend
  ledger, with the NEAR Intents area at the back. A recorded-data card shows the TVL. Next is open.

### Step 2: Native USDC and a bridge's copy
- **Caption:** Two tokens are called USDC on NEAR. Only one is issued by Circle.
- **Explain:** A stablecoin is a token meant to stay worth one dollar. Circle issues USDC on NEAR itself,
  so no bridge stands between you and Circle. USDC.e is a copy a bridge mints when USDC is locked on
  Ethereum: it is only as safe as that bridge (lesson 4.3). They are different tokens.
- **Action:** Button "Compare the two USDCs" (or pick the mailbox). Shows: native USDC 79.3 million,
  bridged USDC.e 14.6 million, recorded on 13 Nov 2026.
- **Gate:** opens once compared.

### Step 3: Read the oracle
- **Caption:** A contract cannot open a website. How does it learn NEAR's price?
- **Explain:** An oracle is a service that writes outside data, like prices, onto the chain, signed. Pyth
  works by pull: an app fetches a signed price and sends it with its transaction. So the stored price is as
  old as the last update. Pyth no longer supports NEAR, and the last NEAR price it stored is from August.
- **Action:** Button "Read the oracle" (or pick the oracle booth). Shows, recorded on 13 Nov 2026 from
  `pyth-oracle.near`: stored NEAR/USD $1.8843 ± $0.0021, published 25 Aug 2026; `get_price`, which checks the
  age, gave nothing (null); the market price that day was $3.4956 (DefiLlama).
- **Gate:** opens once read.

### Step 4: One trade, two pools
- **Caption:** Trade USDC in two Rhea pools. Watch the price as the trade grows.
- **Explain:** An AMM (automated market maker) is an exchange where a pool of tokens and a formula set the
  price. A simple pool keeps X × Y the same, so a big trade moves the price against you (slippage). A stable
  pool uses a curve that keeps same-value tokens near 1:1. The quotes are recorded from the pools on 13 Nov
  2026. Nothing is sent.
- **Action:** Buttons "Trade 100 USDC", "Trade 10,000 USDC", "Trade 100,000 USDC" (or pick a pool: each pick
  tries the next size). Shows both pools' output and the simple pool's price per NEAR.
  - 100 USDC: 100.09 USDt, or 28.48 NEAR at $3.5118.
  - 10,000 USDC: 10,007.85 USDt, or 2,818.16 NEAR at $3.5484.
  - 100,000 USDC: 100,010.05 USDt, or 25,762.09 NEAR at $3.8817 (10.9% over the starting $3.5009).
- **Gate:** opens once the 100,000 USDC trade is shown.
- **Evidence:** `{ pair: "USDC/USDt", amountIn: "100000", stableOut: "100010.046253", simpleOutNear: "25762.090317", recordedOn: "2026-11-13", kind: "recorded" }`.

### Step 5: Money passing through
- **Caption:** NEAR Intents settle on NEAR. More money passes through than sits in its pools.
- **Explain:** In stage 4 you saw intents: you say what you want, and solvers compete to fill it, and the
  trade settles on NEAR. DefiLlama counted $176 million of NEAR Intents trades in the 24 hours to 13 Nov
  2026, more than all of NEAR's DeFi TVL. Where each solver finds its liquidity is up to the solver.
- **Action:** Button "Show the flows" (or pick the NEAR Intents area): arrows run from the intents area onto
  the page. Next is open.

## 3. Check

**Q1. What makes native USDC on NEAR different from USDC.e?**
- A. Circle issues it on NEAR itself, so no bridge stands between you and Circle. **(correct)**
- B. It pays interest every day. *Wrong: a stablecoin aims to stay at one dollar; it does not pay interest by itself.*
- C. It is mined by validators. *Wrong: Circle issues it; nobody mines it.*

**Q2. A lending contract reads a NEAR price from an oracle. The price was published three months ago. What should it do?**
- A. Refuse it as too old, and wait for a fresh signed update. **(correct)**
- B. Use it: an oracle price is always current. *Wrong: a pull oracle's price is as old as its last update.*
- C. Fetch the price from a website instead. *Wrong: a contract cannot make web requests.*

**Q3. Why did 100,000 USDC trade near 1:1 in the stable pool but cost 10.9% more per NEAR in the simple pool?**
- A. A stable pool's curve keeps same-value tokens near 1:1; a simple pool's price moves as a big trade drains one side. **(correct)**
- B. The simple pool charges a 10.9% fee. *Wrong: its fee is 0.3%; the rest is the price moving.*
- C. The stable pool is bigger. *Wrong: it held about $600,000, less than the simple pool's $1.9 million.*

## 4. Honest take

**Strengths:**
- **Native dollars.** Circle and Tether issue USDC and USDt on NEAR itself, with no bridge in between.
  Native USDC (79.3 million) is over five times bridged USDC.e (14.6 million). [circle-usdc] [near-rpc]
- **Stable pools work.** 100,000 USDC traded within 0.01% of 1:1 in a pool of about $600,000. [near-rpc]
- **Money moves through.** NEAR Intents settled $176 million of trades in one day, more than all of NEAR's
  DeFi TVL. [defillama-intents]

**Weaknesses:**
- **Shallow pools.** NEAR's DeFi TVL is about $162 million; Ethereum's is about $39.4 billion. 100,000 USDC
  into NEAR's biggest USDC/NEAR pool paid 10.9% over the starting price. [defillama-chains] [near-rpc]
- **Concentrated.** One group, Rhea, runs the main exchange and the main lending market: about $160 million
  of the $162 million. [defillama-protocols]
- **Oracles can go quiet.** Pyth no longer supports NEAR, and its stored NEAR price is from August.
  Protocols must check a price's age and find other feeds. [pyth-docs] [near-rpc]
- **Two USDCs.** Bridged USDC.e still circulates next to native USDC, and they are not the same token.
  [near-rpc]

Compared with: **Ethereum** (about $39.4 billion in DeFi, the deepest pools) and **Solana** (about $5.0
billion). Recorded on 13 Nov 2026 (DefiLlama).
