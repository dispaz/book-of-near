## In one sentence

NEAR's financial infrastructure centers around native stablecoins, automated market makers (Ref Finance / Rhea), collateralized money markets (Burrow), and high-frequency oracle networks (Pyth), unified by cross-chain intent settlement.

## The problem it solves

A Layer 1 blockchain is an empty sandbox without deep, accessible liquidity and dependable market data. Decentralized applications (lending, perps, prediction markets, and AI agents) require accurate real-time asset pricing, liquid stablecoins that maintain dollar pegs, and automated exchange venues to facilitate commerce. [near-defi]

## How it works on NEAR

**Native Stablecoins (USDC & USDT).**
- Unlike chains that rely exclusively on bridged, synthetic IOUs, NEAR hosts native, canonical **USDC** (issued directly by Circle) and **USDT** (issued by Tether). [near-stablecoins]
- Eliminates bridge counterparty risk for dollar-denominated assets. [near-stablecoins]

**Automated Market Makers (DEXs: Ref Finance / Rhea).**
- The flagship decentralized exchange on NEAR. [ref-finance]
- Supports standard Uniswap-style $x \times y = k$ constant product pools, Curve-style stable-swap pools (for low-slippage trading between pegged assets like USDT/USDC), and concentrated liquidity pools. [ref-finance]

**Lending & Money Markets (Burrow).**
- Over-collateralized borrowing and lending protocol. [burrow-cash]
- Users deposit interest-bearing assets (NEAR, stNEAR, USDC) to earn yield or borrow against collateral, with liquidations triggered automatically if collateral ratios drop below safety thresholds. [burrow-cash]

**Decentralized Oracles (Pyth Network).**
- Smart contracts on NEAR cannot make external HTTP calls to fetch Web2 stock or crypto prices directly. [pyth-network]
- The Pyth Network publishes sub-second, institutional-grade price feeds on-chain via a "pull oracle" model: users or transactions pull signed price updates onto NEAR exactly when needed, paying tiny gas fees only upon update. [pyth-network]

**The Intent Settlement Nexus.**
- As explored in Stage 4, NEAR Intents and the Defuse protocol sit on top of this DeFi layer, allowing external capital from Ethereum, Solana, and Bitcoin to tap into NEAR liquidity pools effortlessly. [near-intents]

## Words to know

- **AMM (Automated Market Maker):** A decentralized exchange protocol relying on mathematical pricing formulas rather than order books.
- **Stable-swap:** A specialized AMM algorithm optimized for assets trading at a 1:1 parity (e.g. USDC and USDT), delivering near-zero slippage.
- **Oracle:** A secure bridge that feeds real-world financial data (like exchange rates) into on-chain smart contracts.
- **Collateral Ratio:** The ratio of deposited assets to borrowed assets required to keep a loan solvent.
- **Native Stablecoin:** A token minted directly by the underlying issuer (Circle/Tether) on the blockchain rather than bridged by a third party.

## Common misunderstandings

- **"Smart contracts can fetch Yahoo Finance or CoinGecko prices directly."** Blockchains are deterministic sandboxes; smart contracts cannot execute outbound internet HTTP calls. They must read price data delivered by oracles. [pyth-network]
- **"All stablecoins on NEAR are bridged from Ethereum."** Native USDC and USDT exist directly on NEAR, backed 1:1 by reserves held by Circle and Tether. [near-stablecoins]

## Compared with Ethereum & Solana

- **DEX Architecture:** Ethereum has Uniswap; Solana has Raydium; NEAR has Ref Finance. While EVM DEXs suffer from expensive sandwich attacks (MEV), NEAR's sub-second block finality and private intent relays mitigate MEV extraction. [ref-finance]
- **Oracle Latency:** Traditional oracles (like early Chainlink) pushed updates periodically at high gas cost. Modern pull oracles like Pyth on NEAR update on demand with sub-second accuracy. [pyth-network]

## Strengths and weaknesses (feeds the Honest take)

**Strengths:**
- Native dollar liquidity: Canonical USDC and USDT eliminate bridge collapse risks. [near-stablecoins]
- Low gas trading: Swapping on an AMM on NEAR costs a fraction of a cent in gas, compared to $5–$50 on Ethereum L1. [ref-finance]
- Sub-second oracles: Pyth provides institutional-grade market data refreshed continuously. [pyth-network]

**Weaknesses:**
- Liquidity depth: While growing, total DeFi TVL (Total Value Locked) is smaller than Ethereum mainnet, meaning large $10M+ single-trade whale swaps may experience slippage without intent routing. [near-defi]
- Ecosystem fragmentation: Multiple competing wrapped token representations from older bridges (e.g. `USDC.e`) still circulate alongside native USDC. [near-stablecoins]
