## In one sentence

On NEAR, dollars come as stablecoins issued on NEAR itself (Circle's USDC, Tether's USDt), they trade in
Rhea's pools (Rhea is the merger of Ref Finance and Burrow), they are lent and borrowed in Rhea Lend, and
contracts learn prices from oracles; cross-chain trades from NEAR Intents settle on NEAR too.

## The problem it solves

A chain with accounts and contracts is not yet an economy. People need money that holds its value
(stablecoins), places to swap one token for another (exchanges), places to lend and borrow, and a way for
contracts to know prices (oracles). DeFi (decentralized finance) builds these as smart contracts that
anyone can use without a bank in the middle. [defillama-protocols]

## How it works on NEAR

**Native stablecoins (USDC and USDt).**
- Circle lists NEAR as a chain where it issues USDC itself, at the account
  `17208628f84f5d6ad33f0da3bbbeb27ffcb398eac501a31bd6ad2011e36133a1` (mainnet). [circle-usdc]
- Tether lists NEAR among the protocols it issues USDt on, at the account `usdt.tether-token.near`.
  [tether-protocols] It has been there since September 2022, when Bitfinex opened USDt-on-NEAR deposits and
  withdrawals. [tether-near] On chain, `usdt.tether-token.near` names its
  token "Tether USD" (symbol `USDt`). [near-rpc]
- A bridged token is different: `a0b86991c6218b36c1d19d4a2e9eb0ce3606eb48.factory.bridge.near` names itself
  "Bridged USDC" (`USDC.e`). It is minted by a bridge contract when USDC is locked on Ethereum (lesson 4.3's
  lock-and-mint). [near-rpc] [near-bridge-risk]
- So a native coin has no bridge between you and the issuer; a bridged coin is only as safe as its bridge.
  (Lesson 4.3 covers the bridge hacks.) [near-bridge-risk]

**Exchanges (Rhea Dex, formerly Ref Finance).**
- DefiLlama lists "Rhea Dex" (previous name "Ref Finance") and describes Rhea Finance as the merger of Ref
  Finance and Burrow Finance. [defillama-protocols] The exchange contract is still `v2.ref-finance.near`.
  [near-rpc]
- An AMM (automated market maker) is an exchange where a contract holds a pool of two or more tokens and a
  formula sets the price, with no order book. [rhea-sdk]
- A **simple pool** uses the constant-product rule: `out = in·(1 − fee)·Y / (X + in·(1 − fee))`, so
  `X × Y` stays the same. The bigger the trade compared with the pool, the worse the price. [rhea-sdk]
  (`swap.ts`: `amount_with_fee * out_balance / (FEE_DIVISOR * in_balance + amount_with_fee)`.)
- **Stable pools** (kinds `STABLE_SWAP`, `RATED_SWAP`, `DEGEN_SWAP`) use the StableSwap curve with an
  amplification factor (`amp`), which keeps tokens of the same value trading close to 1:1 until the pool
  is badly out of balance. [rhea-sdk] (`types.ts`: `StablePoolKind = 'STABLE_SWAP' | 'RATED_SWAP' |
  'DEGEN_SWAP'`; `stable-swap.ts`: `calc_d(amp, …)`.)
- `DEGEN_SWAP` (the kind of pool #6416 below) is a variant of StableSwap: like `RATED_SWAP`, it prices each
  token with a rate (how many dollars one token is worth) before running the StableSwap curve, instead of
  assuming every token is worth exactly the same. [rhea-sdk] The lesson calls it "a stable pool" and does
  not teach rates.
- Fees are in basis points of 10,000 (`FEE_DIVISOR = 10000`): `total_fee` 30 is 0.3%, 1 is 0.01%. [rhea-sdk]

**Lending (Rhea Lend, formerly Burrow).**
- DefiLlama: "Rhea Lending is a decentralized, non-custodial, pool-based interest rates platform that enables
  users to supply assets to earn interest, and to borrow against them to unlock liquidity." Previous name:
  Burrow. [defillama-protocols]

**Oracles (Pyth and others).**
- A smart contract cannot call a web API: it only sees what is on chain. An oracle is a service that
  writes outside data (like prices) onto the chain, signed so contracts can check where it came from.
  [pyth-docs]
- Pyth works by pull: "the application fetches a signed update and submits it with the transaction". The
  price on chain is only as fresh as the last update someone pulled. [pyth-docs]
- Pyth Core **no longer supports NEAR**: Pyth's NEAR page says Pyth Core "also no longer supports … NEAR".
  [pyth-docs] (The contract-addresses page separately says the contracts of the chains it lists "weren't
  upgraded on August 26, 2026". That sentence is about those listed chains, so the lesson does not quote it
  for NEAR. [pyth-addresses]) The lesson says only that NEAR's last stored Pyth price is from August
  (below).
- What that looks like on chain (13 Nov 2026, NEAR mainnet `pyth-oracle.near`, feed NEAR/USD
  `c415de8d…6750`): `get_price_unsafe` still returns the last stored price, $1.8843 ± $0.0021, published
  25 Aug 2026 23:07 UTC; `get_price`, the call that checks the price's age, returns `null`. [near-rpc]
  The same day, DefiLlama's price for NEAR (from CoinGecko) was $3.4956. [defillama-coins]
- The lesson: a price has an age. A contract must check it, and refuse an old one. An oracle is a service a
  protocol depends on, and it can stop.
- Which oracle each NEAR protocol uses today is (unverified) and the lesson does not say.

**NEAR Intents.**
- As in stage 4: a user signs what they want, solvers compete to fill it, and the trade settles on NEAR
  (the `intents.near` contract). [near-intents]
- DefiLlama counts NEAR Intents as DEX volume: $176.4 million in the 24 hours to 13 Nov 2026, $3.77 billion
  in 30 days. Assets held in it on NEAR: $88.7 million. [defillama-intents] [defillama-protocols]
- Where solvers get their liquidity (NEAR pools, other chains, their own funds) is up to each solver: which
  share comes from Rhea's pools is (unverified) and the lesson does not say.

## Words to know

- **DeFi (decentralized finance):** money services (swaps, loans) run by smart contracts anyone can use.
- **Stablecoin:** a token meant to stay worth one dollar, because its issuer holds reserves and buys it back.
- **Native stablecoin:** one the issuer (Circle, Tether) mints on that chain itself, not a bridge's copy.
- **AMM (automated market maker):** an exchange where a pool and a formula set the price, not an order book.
- **Slippage:** how much worse your price is than the pool's price before your trade.
- **Stable pool:** a pool whose curve keeps same-value tokens trading near 1:1.
- **TVL (total value locked):** the value of the tokens deposited in DeFi contracts.
- **Oracle:** a service that writes outside data, like prices, onto the chain, signed.

## Common misunderstandings

- **"Smart contracts can fetch prices from a website."** They cannot make web requests; they read prices an
  oracle put on chain. [pyth-docs]
- **"An oracle's price is always current."** A pull oracle's stored price is as old as its last update. On
  13 Nov 2026 the NEAR/USD price stored on `pyth-oracle.near` was from 25 Aug 2026. [near-rpc]
- **"USDC.e is the same as USDC."** USDC.e is a bridge's token, a separate contract with its own supply.
  [near-rpc]
- **"Every swap on an AMM is close to the market price."** Only while the trade is small next to the pool.
  [near-rpc]

- **Depth:** DefiLlama chain TVL on 13 Nov 2026: Ethereum about $39.4 billion, Solana about $5.0 billion,

- **Same building blocks:** Ethereum and Solana also have AMMs, lending markets, stablecoins and oracles.

## Strengths and weaknesses (feeds the Honest take)

**Strengths:**
- Native dollars: Circle and Tether issue USDC and USDt on NEAR itself, with no bridge in between; native USDC
  supply (79.3 million) is over five times bridged USDC.e (14.6 million). [circle-usdc] [near-rpc]
- Stable pools work: 100,000 USDC traded only about 0.01% away from 1:1 in a pool of about $600,000.
  [near-rpc]
- Money moves through NEAR: NEAR Intents settled $176 million of trades in one day, more than all of NEAR's
  DeFi TVL. [defillama-intents]

**Weaknesses:**
- Shallow pools: NEAR's DeFi TVL is about $162 million, against Ethereum's $39.4 billion. A 100,000 USDC buy
  in Rhea's biggest USDC/NEAR pool paid 10.9% over the starting price. A router can split a big trade
  across several pools, so that figure is for one pool only. [defillama-chains] [near-rpc]
- Concentrated: one group, Rhea, runs both the main exchange and the main lending market, about $160 million
  of the $162 million. [defillama-protocols]
- Oracles can go quiet: Pyth Core no longer supports NEAR, and its stored NEAR/USD price is from August.
  Protocols must check a price's age and find other feeds. [pyth-docs] [near-rpc]
- Two USDCs: bridged USDC.e still circulates next to native USDC, and they are not the same token.
  [near-rpc]
