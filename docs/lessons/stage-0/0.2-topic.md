## In one sentence

A crypto wallet does not hold your coins—it holds your private keys, which sign messages to authorize transfers of coins that live permanently on the blockchain ledger.

## The problem it solves

In traditional finance, banks hold your money and authenticate you with passwords. If the bank fails or freezes your account, you lose access. In decentralized blockchains, you must have a way to prove ownership and authorize actions without an intermediary holding custody. [near-keys]

## How it works

**Asymmetric Cryptography (Key Pairs).**
- Every account identity begins with a **key pair**:
  1. **Private Key:** A secret number that only you know. It creates cryptographic digital signatures. [near-keys]
  2. **Public Key:** Derived mathematically from the private key. Anyone can see it and use it to verify that a signature came from your private key without revealing the secret. [near-keys]

**Seed Phrases (BIP-39).**
- Because a raw cryptographic key is a random string of 32 or 64 bytes (nearly impossible for humans to memorize or write without typos), wallets use a **mnemonic seed phrase** (typically 12 or 24 standard dictionary words). [bip-39]
- Using standard derivation paths (SLIP-10 / BIP-44), a seed phrase deterministically reconstructs the exact same private key every time. [bip-39]

**How Addresses are Derived.**
- **Implicit Account on NEAR:** On NEAR, an initial implicit address is 64 hex characters—the exact hex representation of the ed25519 public key. [near-account-id]
- **Ethereum Address:** Computed from a secp256k1 public key by taking the Keccak-256 hash and keeping the last 20 bytes (prefixed with `0x`). [eth-accounts]
- **Named Account on NEAR:** A custom readable name (e.g. `ana.testnet`) created via a transaction that registers the public key on-chain (explored in Stage 1). [near-account-id]

**Signing & Verification.**
- To send coins, the wallet signs a transaction message with the private key, producing a digital signature. [near-keys]
- Nodes verify: `verify(message, signature, publicKey) === true`.
- If an attacker tampers with even one letter of the message or tries to forge a signature, verification immediately returns `false`. [near-keys]

## Words to know

- **Private Key:** The secret cryptographic key that generates signatures. Never share it.
- **Public Key:** The public half of a key pair used by others to verify signatures.
- **Seed Phrase (Mnemonic):** A 12- or 24-word phrase used to deterministically generate key pairs.
- **Implicit Account:** An account whose address is the raw hex encoding of its public key.
- **Digital Signature:** A mathematical proof that a specific message was authorized by the holder of a private key.

## Common misunderstandings

- **"My wallet stores my crypto tokens."** No. Your tokens live on the public blockchain ledger; your wallet stores the private keys that prove you own those tokens. [near-keys]
- **"If I lose my seed phrase, customer support can recover it."** Blockchains are self-custodial. There is no customer support or password reset mechanism for lost private keys. [near-keys]
- **"Changing a letter in a signed message leaves the signature intact."** Any alteration to the signed data breaks the mathematical signature completely. [near-keys]

## Compared with Ethereum & Solana

- **Key Curves:** NEAR primarily uses **ed25519** (high speed, small 32-byte keys, resistance to side-channel attacks), matching Solana, but also natively supports secp256k1 (used by Bitcoin and Ethereum). [near-keys]
- **Account Separation:** On Ethereum, an account is permanently bound to its single secp256k1 key. On NEAR, accounts are distinct entities that can hold multiple keys and rotate them over time. [near-keys]

## Strengths and weaknesses (feeds the Honest take)

**Strengths:**
- True self-custody: You have sole control over your funds without third-party counterparty risk. [near-keys]
- Standardized recovery: BIP-39 mnemonic phrases allow moving keys between compatible wallet applications. [bip-39]
- Modern curve: ed25519 delivers faster signing and smaller signatures than legacy secp256k1. [near-keys]

**Weaknesses:**
- Unforgiving security: If you reveal your seed phrase to a scammer, your account is drained instantly with zero recourse. [near-keys]
- Hex complexity: Implicit 64-character hex addresses are prone to human transcription errors. [near-account-id]

## What the lesson must show

1. Security gate warning: Remind the learner never to enter a real seed phrase into a website.
2. Generate a fresh 12-word seed phrase and derive its ed25519 private key.
3. Show the derived public key and its implicit 64-hex NEAR address.
4. Compare with an Ethereum address (42 hex characters from secp256k1) and a named NEAR account.
5. Sign a text message, verify it passes, then tamper with one character and observe verification fail.
6. Delete/throw away the throwaway keys safely.
