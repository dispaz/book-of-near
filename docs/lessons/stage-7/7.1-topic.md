# Topic 7.1: Capstone — Tracing a Cross-Chain Intent End-to-End

file says. Facts carry a source tag; anything else is marked (unverified).

## In one sentence

The capstone synthesizes every stage of Book of NEAR by tracing one cross-chain intent end-to-end: from named accounts and scoped keys, across asynchronous receipts and Nightshade shards, through MPC Chain Signatures, to atomic solver settlement.

## The problem it solves

Most blockchain education leaves learners with fragmented concepts—they understand keys in isolation, sharding in theory, or smart contracts in a sandbox, but cannot conceptualize how these distinct protocol layers coordinate to execute modern multichain applications. [near-curriculum]

## How it works on NEAR

The complete lifecycle of a cross-chain intent unites all architectural layers of the protocol:

```
[Stage 1: Named Account & Scoped Key]
              │
              ▼ (Transaction)
[Stage 2: Nightshade Shards & Asynchronous Receipts]
              │
              ▼ (Action / Storage)
[Stage 3: Wasm Execution, Storage Staking & 30% Gas Rebate]
              │
              ▼ (Intent Settlement & MPC)
[Stage 4: Solver Settlement & MPC Chain Signatures]
              │
              ▼
[Stage 7: Final Multichain Execution & NFT Badge Mint]
```

1. **Identity & Authorization (Stage 1):**
   - The learner's human-readable account (`learner.testnet`) uses a scoped **function-call key** limited to the intent verifier contract. The learner's root private key remains safe and cold. [near-keys]

2. **Network Routing & Sharding (Stage 2):**
   - The transaction enters the chunk of the signer's **shard** (determined by boundary accounts).
   - Execution emits an **Action Receipt** that hops across block boundaries to the intent contract's shard, verified statelessly by validators using state witnesses. [near-sharding]

3. **Smart Contract Execution & Gas (Stage 3):**
   - The intent verifier smart contract executes inside the **WebAssembly** runtime.
   - Its storage is backed by refundable **storage staking** (NEP-145).
   - 70% of gas spent is burned, while **30% goes to the contract's account** (the account the code is deployed on, not a separate developer balance). [near-gas]

4. **Chain Abstraction & Settlement (Stage 4):**
   - The open **Solver Network** (Defuse) matches the intent.
   - Solvers deliver native assets on the destination blockchain (e.g. Ethereum or Arbitrum).
   - If a cross-chain transaction signature is required, the decentralized **MPC network** (`v1.signer`) coordinates threshold key shares to sign the target-chain transaction. [near-chain-sigs]
   - A 1Click swap whose output stays in the learner's NEAR Intents account (`intents.near`) needs no chain signature: the solver pays out and the verifier settles inside NEAR. The USDC there is a bridged token (`…omft.near`), so "without bridges" means without *user-facing* bridges. [spike-6]

5. **Completion & Proof of Competence (Stage 7):**
   - The final execution receipt resolves on NEAR.
   - Evidence is validated by the badge signer, unlocking the **Book of NEAR NFT Badge**. [near-badges]

## Words to know

- **Full-Stack Composition:** The integration of identity, sharding, compute, and multichain settlement into a single seamless user flow.
- **Intent Lifecycle:** The chronological journey of an intent from user signing to solver execution and on-chain verification.
- **Proof of Competence:** On-chain cryptographic attestation (NFT badge) certifying a learner's verified completion of protocol milestones.

## Common misunderstandings

- **"Smart contracts and cross-chain operations happen on different networks."** On NEAR, the same account identity and sharded protocol orchestrate both local contract execution and foreign-chain signing seamlessly. [near-chain-sigs]
- **"Sharded chains cannot coordinate atomic cross-chain operations."** NEAR's receipt model combined with intent verifiers provides deterministic cross-chain settlement without requiring synchronous global execution locks. [near-intents]

## Compared with Ethereum & Monolithic L1s

- **Ethereum + L2 Ecosystem:** Requires navigating disparate rollups, third-party bridges, complex ERC-4337 bundlers, and multiple native gas tokens. [eth-rollups]
- **NEAR Protocol:** Delivers a unified Chain Abstraction stack where accounts, scoped keys, sharded execution, and cross-chain MPC signatures operate as native protocol primitives. [near-chain-sigs]

## Strengths and weaknesses (feeds the Honest take)

**Strengths:**
- Complete end-to-end chain abstraction: Enables single-click multichain experiences without user-facing bridges or foreign gas friction. [near-intents]
- Composability without monolithic state: Combines sharded scale with asynchronous cross-contract and cross-chain execution. [near-sharding]

**Weaknesses:**
- Multi-component latency: A full pipeline spends a few blocks on cross-shard receipt hops, and a chain signature took 7–14 seconds from send to final on testnet in our tests (not the ~5 s of spike 5). [spike-5, `docs/spikes-seed.md` live check of 11 Nov 2026]
- Architectural cognitive overhead: Understanding the complete system requires grasping asynchronous receipts rather than familiar synchronous call stacks. [near-cross-contract]
