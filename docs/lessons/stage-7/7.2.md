## 1. Learning goals

1. Compare NEAR with Ethereum, Solana and Cosmos on four dimensions: accounts, scalability, speed and atomicity, liquidity.
2. Say where NEAR leads (accounts and scoped keys, built-in sharding, reaching other chains).
3. Say where NEAR trails (liquidity depth, atomic calls across contracts, mindshare).

## 2. Steps

### Step 1: No perfect blockchain
- **Caption:** Every chain says it is the best. In engineering, every choice is a trade-off.
- **Explain:** No blockchain wins at everything. One chain (Solana) needs big validator machines; rollups (Ethereum) split money across chains; shards (NEAR) make calls between contracts non-atomic.
- **Scene:** Four pillars of the same height (NEAR, Ethereum, Solana, Cosmos) and four cards lying in front. The panel lists each chain's core idea and trade-off. Next is open.

### Step 2: Dimension 1: accounts
- **Caption:** Readable names and scoped keys, or hex addresses and contract wallets?
- **Explain:** On NEAR, account names and function-call keys are part of the protocol. On Ethereum, smart-account features need contract wallets and extra services called bundlers (ERC-4337). Open the Accounts card to see the board.
- **Action:** "Open the Accounts card" (dock button, or pick the card). NEAR leads; Ethereum and Solana are trade-offs; Cosmos is not compared (no source).

### Step 3: Dimension 2: scalability
- **Caption:** Shards, rollups, or one big state?
- **Explain:** NEAR splits state and work across shards. Ethereum's plan adds data space for rollups instead of shards that run code. Solana runs one large shared state, which asks a lot of validator hardware. Cosmos adds whole new chains.
- **Action:** "Open the Scalability card". NEAR leads (shards; testnet had 10 when checked); Ethereum (danksharding adds data space for rollups, not execution shards), Solana (Anza lists 12 cores / 24 threads or more for a validator) and Cosmos (200+ chains linked by IBC, each with its own validators, so security is split; marked unverified) are trade-offs.

### Step 4: Dimension 3: speed and atomicity
- **Caption:** Here NEAR accepts a real trade-off.
- **Explain:** Solana runs a call across many accounts in one atomic (all-or-nothing) transaction. On NEAR, a call to another contract travels as a receipt (a message) and runs in a later block, so a chain of calls takes several blocks and cannot roll back as one. Within one function call nothing can re-enter, but other calls can run between a call and its callback. So a contract must update its state before it makes the call.
- **Action:** "Open the Speed card". Solana leads (a call across many accounts is one atomic transaction; ~400 ms blocks, marked unverified; no "fastest" ranking); NEAR (0.6 to 1 s blocks, final after about 1.2 s; receipts across blocks, no rollback across hops; other calls can run before a callback, so state is updated before the call) and Ethereum (12 s slots, atomic calls, reentrancy) are trade-offs; Cosmos not compared (each chain sets its own block time).

### Step 5: Dimension 4: liquidity
- **Caption:** Ethereum is far ahead on money locked in DeFi.
- **Explain:** Liquidity is how much money is ready to trade. Ethereum holds the most by far. NEAR's own DeFi is much smaller, which is why NEAR builds chain abstraction: NEAR Intents and Chain Signatures let a NEAR account use other chains' liquidity. The figures were recorded on one day; they are not live.
- **Action:** "Open the Liquidity card". Recorded DefiLlama TVL: Ethereum about $39.4 billion (leads), Solana about $5.0 billion (trade-off), NEAR about $162 million (trails; it routes to other chains' liquidity with NEAR Intents and Chain Signatures); Cosmos not compared.
- **Evidence:** once the learner has opened all four cards: `{ scoreboardReviewed: "true", recordedOn: "13 Nov 2026" }`. (The learner picks no favourite, so none is recorded.)

## 3. Check

**Q1. What is the main trade-off of NEAR's sharded design?**
- A. Calls between contracts are asynchronous: they take several blocks and do not roll back as one. **(correct)**
- B. Transactions can never be private. *Wrong: privacy is a separate question; what sharding trades away is atomic calls across contracts.*
- C. The chain must stop every Sunday for maintenance. *Wrong: NEAR runs all the time.*

**Q2. Where does Ethereum hold a large lead over NEAR?**
- A. Money locked in DeFi (TVL), institutional capital and developer attention. **(correct)**
- B. Lower fees on its base chain. *Wrong: high base-chain fees are the trade-off Ethereum accepts.*
- C. Readable account names built into the protocol. *Wrong: Ethereum accounts are 0x hex addresses.*

**Q3. Why does Solana ask a lot of its validators' hardware?**
- A. Every validator runs one large shared state, with no shards to split the work. **(correct)**
- B. It uses proof-of-work mining rigs. *Wrong: Solana is proof of stake.*
- C. It runs on quantum computers. *Wrong: its validators are ordinary, if large, servers.*

## 4. Honest take

**Strengths:**
- **Onboarding:** readable account names and scoped keys are part of the protocol, with no contract wallet needed. [near-keys] [near-account-id]
- **Reaching other chains:** Chain Signatures and NEAR Intents let a NEAR account use other chains' liquidity. If that is your bottleneck, NEAR fits. [near-intents]
- **Built-in sharding:** NEAR adds capacity by adding shards, and validators check chunks without storing every shard. [near-sharding]

**Weaknesses:**
- **No free lunch:** shards make calls between contracts asynchronous. They take several blocks and do not roll back as one. [near-cross-contract]
- **Much less money:** on 13 Nov 2026 DefiLlama counted about $162 million locked in NEAR DeFi, against about $39.4 billion on Ethereum and about $5.0 billion on Solana. (The code builds this line from `board.ts`: `TVL` and `RECORDED_ON`.) [defillama]
- **Network effects:** Ethereum's capital and Solana's retail trading are hard to compete with. NEAR needs builders to keep choosing it. [bon-spec]

Compared with: **Ethereum**, **Solana**, and **Cosmos**.
