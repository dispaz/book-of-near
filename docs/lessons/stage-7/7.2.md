## 1. Learning goals

1. Compare NEAR objectively against Ethereum, Solana, and Cosmos across 4 technical dimensions.
2. Identify where NEAR outperforms competitors (accounts, scoped keys, developer rebates, chain signatures).
3. Identify where NEAR trails competitors (liquidity depth, synchronous call atomicity, ecosystem mindshare).

## 2. Steps

### Step 1: The myth of the perfect blockchain
- **Caption:** Every crypto marketing campaign claims to be the best. In engineering, every choice is a trade-off.
- **Explain:** No blockchain wins at everything. If you choose monolithic execution like Solana, you need monster validator hardware. If you choose rollups like Ethereum, you fragment liquidity. If you choose sharding like NEAR, you trade away synchronous cross-contract atomicity.
- **Scene:** An interactive scale balancing Ethereum, Solana, Cosmos, and NEAR. Next is open.

### Step 2: Dimension 1: Accounts and user experience
- **Caption:** Named accounts and scoped keys vs hex addresses and smart contract wallets.
- **Explain:** On NEAR, account names and scoped function-call keys are native protocol primitives. On Ethereum, account abstraction requires complex ERC-4337 bundlers and contract wallets.
- **Action:** Toggle comparison criteria "Accounts & Onboarding". NEAR lights up gold; Ethereum and Solana show trade-off notes.

### Step 3: Dimension 2: Scalability and sharding
- **Caption:** Dynamic Nightshade sharding vs Ethereum L2 rollups vs Solana single state.
- **Explain:** NEAR splits state and execution across shards statelessly. Ethereum gave up on shard chains to focus on rollup data availability. Solana runs a single massive state engine.
- **Action:** Toggle "Scalability Architecture". Review validator requirements and chunk throughput.

### Step 4: Dimension 3: Speed, receipts, and atomicity
- **Caption:** Where NEAR accepts a real trade-off: asynchronous receipts vs single-call execution.
- **Explain:** Solana achieves 400ms synchronous blocks. On NEAR, cross-contract calls hop across blocks as receipts (~1s per hop). You avoid reentrancy bugs, but multi-contract calls take multiple blocks.
- **Action:** Toggle "Execution & Latency". Solana lights up green for raw speed; NEAR highlights the asynchronous trade-off.

### Step 5: Dimension 4: Liquidity and network effects
- **Caption:** Capital depth: Ethereum is the undisputed king of DeFi TVL.
- **Explain:** Ethereum holds billions in deep institutional liquidity. NEAR's native DeFi is smaller, which is why NEAR focuses on Chain Abstraction—allowing users to tap into Ethereum and Solana liquidity without leaving NEAR.
- **Action:** Toggle "Liquidity & Ecosystem". Ethereum leads in TVL; NEAR leads in multichain interoperability.
- **Evidence:** `{ scoreboardReviewed: true, favoriteTradeoff: "chain-abstraction" }`.

## 3. Check

**Q1. What is the fundamental trade-off of NEAR's sharded architecture?**
- A. Cross-contract calls are asynchronous and take multiple blocks to finalize. **(correct)**
- B. Transactions can never be made private. *Wrong: confidential intents provide privacy.*
- C. The blockchain must shut down every Sunday for maintenance. *Wrong: NEAR runs 24/7 continuously.*

**Q2. Where does Ethereum hold a massive, decisive advantage over NEAR?**
- A. Total Value Locked (TVL), institutional capital depth, and ecosystem mindshare. **(correct)**
- B. Lower gas fees for retail users. *Wrong: Ethereum L1 gas fees are substantially higher.*
- C. Human-readable account names natively built into the base protocol. *Wrong: ENS is an external smart contract layer.*

**Q3. Why does Solana require significantly higher-spec validator hardware than NEAR?**
- A. It processes a single massive global state concurrently without sharding. **(correct)**
- B. It uses proof-of-work mining rigs. *Wrong: Solana is Proof of Stake / Proof of History.*
- C. It runs on quantum computers. *Wrong: Solana runs on standard enterprise servers.*

## 4. Honest take

**Strengths:**
- **Objective clarity:** Understanding real trade-offs prevents costly architecture mistakes when building dApps. [bon-spec]
- **Targeted advantages:** NEAR excels where user onboarding (names, keys) and cross-chain interaction (Chain Signatures, Intents) are the primary bottlenecks. [near-chain-sigs]

**Weaknesses:**
- **No free lunch:** Sharded scaling inherently increases the complexity of cross-contract coordination. [near-sharding]
- **Market competition:** Competing against established network effects (Ethereum) and retail trading momentum (Solana) requires continuous developer adoption. [bon-spec]

Compared with: **Ethereum**, **Solana**, and **Cosmos**.
