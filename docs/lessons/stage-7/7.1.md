# Lesson 7.1: Trace one intent end to end

Issue: #26. Target time: 6 minutes. Chain access: none, a replay (works the same in demo and testnet mode; sends nothing). Views: 2D and 3D, one shared state.

Facts come from `docs/lessons/stage-7/7.1-topic.md`. Anything else is marked (unverified).

## 1. Learning goals

1. Trace one cross-chain intent through every layer of NEAR: named account, scoped key, shards and receipts, Wasm execution, and solver settlement (with Chain Signatures where a payout must leave NEAR).
2. See how the earlier stages fit together into one chain-abstraction flow. Each stop links back to the lesson that taught it.
3. Record the capstone completion evidence (used later by the final exam and badge).

The replay uses the learner's own results where they have them (their 4.2 swap, 1.1 name, 2.1 shards and so on), else lesson 4.2's recorded quote.

## 2. Steps

### Step 1: One engine
- **Caption:** Every concept you learned is part of one engine. Watch them work together to move money across chains.
- **Explain:** You started with ledgers and keys. Now you replay one cross-chain intent through five layers: account, scoped key, shards, smart contract and Chain Signatures. Each stop links back to the lesson that taught it. Where you did that lesson, the replay uses your own results.
- **Scene:** The whole trail on one page: your mailbox and key, two shard ribbons, the verifier scroll, the signing nodes and where the USDC lands. Next is open.

### Step 2: Layers 1 and 2: account and scoped key
- **Caption:** Layers 1 and 2: your named account ({name}) signs the intent with a function-call key.
- **Explain:** You do not risk your full-access key. A function-call key (Stage 1) may call only one contract, here the intent verifier, and its gas allowance is capped. The signed intent is a transaction, like the one you sent in lesson 0.3.
- **Action:** Button "Authorize intent" (or pick the mailbox or the key). The stamped parcel leaves your mailbox. Next unlocks when signed.

### Step 3: Layer 3: shards and receipts
- **Caption:** Layer 3: the parcel enters your shard and becomes a receipt that moves on in a later block.
- **Explain:** NEAR is split into shards (Nightshade). Your transaction is processed on your account's shard. It creates a receipt, a message to the verifier contract, which runs on the verifier's shard in a later block. Validators check each chunk with a state witness, so no shard waits for a global lock.
- **Scene:** The receipt slip hops from your shard to the verifier's shard. Next is open.

### Step 4: Layer 4: the Wasm contract and gas
- **Caption:** Layer 4: the contract runs as Wasm, and the NEAR paid for its gas is destroyed.
- **Explain:** The verifier contract checks the intent's terms. It is WebAssembly (Wasm) code, like the contract you deployed in Stage 3. Its storage is paid for with staked NEAR that can be returned (storage staking). The NEAR paid for the gas the call uses is destroyed. Up to and including protocol version 86 (a version of NEAR's rules), the contract's account got 30% of it; from version 87, none.
- **Scene:** The verifier scroll's gear turns. The facts show the gas: all burned, none to the contract. Next is open.

### Step 5: Layer 5: the solver settles
- **Caption:** Layer 5: a solver fills the order. Chain Signatures sign only if a payout must leave NEAR.
- **Explain:** Solvers compete to fill the intent; 1Click shows only the winning quote. The winner pays out from its own funds, and the verifier contract settles the trade. A real swap from this app keeps the USDC in your NEAR Intents account (intents.near), not on Arbitrum, so no chain signature is needed. Only when a NEAR account must sign on another chain does the signing network (the v1.signer contract) sign with key shares, for a 1 yoctoNEAR deposit.
- **Action:** Button "Verify foreign settlement" (or pick where the USDC lands). Plays: the solver pays out → done. No signing phase is played; a side note says the signing nodes sign only when a payout must leave NEAR, and this replay does not use them. Next unlocks when done.
- **Evidence** (only when the learner verifies; opening the lesson at a later step fills the view but reports nothing): `{ capstoneComplete: "true", mode, txHash, intentSource, mpcVerified, stagesTraced, ownLayers }`. `mpcVerified` is "true" only when the learner got a chain signature back in lesson 4.1.

## 3. Check

**Q1. Why does the learner use a function-call key to authorize the intent?**
- A. So that even if the app is compromised, the account's full-access key and main funds stay safe. **(correct)**
- B. Because full-access keys cannot connect to the internet. *Wrong: full-access keys can do everything, which is why you keep them out of apps.*
- C. To avoid paying taxes on crypto. *Wrong: access key types are a security tool, not a tax rule.*

**Q2. How do Nightshade shards handle calls between contracts without locking the blockchain?**
- A. Through receipts that carry the call to the other shard and run in a later block. **(correct)**
- B. By stopping all transactions on all other shards. *Wrong: shards process their chunks in parallel.*
- C. By sending emails between node operators. *Wrong: receipts are routed by the protocol itself.*

**Q3. How does an intent deliver tokens on another chain without user-facing bridges?**
- A. A solver pays out from funds it already holds there, and the verifier contract on NEAR settles the trade. **(correct)**
- B. By printing copies of Bitcoin. *Wrong: the tokens delivered are the real, native ones.*
- C. By shutting down the other chain. *Wrong: other chains run normally.*

## 4. Honest take

Compared with: Ethereum, Solana.

**Strengths:**
- Chain abstraction from end to end: one named account can sign for other chains, and intents swap across chains without user-facing bridges or gas tokens for each chain. [near-intents]
- The parts are built in: named accounts, scoped keys, shards and Chain Signatures are protocol features, not add-ons from different teams. [near-chain-sigs]
- Compared with Ethereum and its rollups (many separate networks, bridges and gas tokens) and Solana (one fast shared state, but no sharding and no built-in signing for other chains). [eth-rollups]

**Weaknesses:**
- Seconds, not one block: a full intent spends a few blocks on receipts, and a chain signature took 7 to 14 seconds on testnet in our tests. [spike-5, live check 11 Nov 2026]
- Many parts to debug: when something fails you trace nonces, receipt hops and solver liquidity, across systems run by different parties. [near-cross-contract]
- Who should care: builders who want one account to work across chains, and users tired of bridges. If you only ever use one chain, a single-chain wallet is simpler.
