# Lesson 1.2: A key that can do only one thing

Issue: #15. Target time: 6 minutes. Chain access: testnet (real transactions from the practice account) or demo mode (DemoChain, same rules). Everything goes through the `Chain` interface (`docs/chain.md`). The "AI agent" is a script: no model is called.

## 1. Learning goals

1. Tell a full-access key from a function-call key.
2. Add a function-call key limited to one contract and a gas allowance, and read both back from the chain.
3. Watch the chain refuse a key that tries to send NEAR, call another contract or add a key.
4. Delete a key and know why that is the way to take back access.

## 2. Steps

### Step 1: Hook

- **Caption:** An assistant wants to act for you. Would you hand it the key to your whole account?
- **Explain:** Your practice account has a **full-access key**. Whoever holds it can do anything: send all your coins, add keys, even delete the account. That is too much to give a helper. NEAR lets an account hold many keys, and some can be limited.
- **Action:** Scene shows the account with its full-access key and a small agent figure holding out a hand. No gate.

### Step 2: The limited key

- **Caption:** A function-call key can call one contract and pay gas up to an allowance. Nothing else.
- **Explain:** A **function-call key** signs only for the contract you name. It cannot send NEAR, attach NEAR to a call, or add keys. Its **allowance** is the most NEAR it may spend on **gas** (the fee for the work a transaction causes). When the allowance runs out, the key stops working.
- **Action:** Scene lists "can" and "cannot" beside the key. No gate.

### Step 3: Add the key

- **Caption:** Set the allowance, then add a key for the agent. It can call only your counter contract.
- **Explain:** One transaction does two things: it puts a small **counter contract** on your account, and it adds the agent's key with that contract as its only target. Then the app reads the key back from the chain, so what you see is what the chain holds. The allowance belongs to the key, not to your balance.
- **Action:** Field: allowance in NEAR (0.01 to 0.5, default 0.1). Button **Add the agent's key**. After it runs, the scene shows the key card: contract, methods ("any method of this contract"), allowance. Gate opens when the key reads back.

### Step 4: Something it is allowed to do

- **Caption:** Ask the agent to count. It signs with its own key, and the chain accepts. Watch the allowance drop.
- **Explain:** The agent is a script that turns your words into a transaction and signs it with the limited key. It calls `increment` on your counter. The call works, and the gas it used comes out of the key's allowance.
- **Action:** Prompt box with the button **Ask the agent**, prefilled "Add one to the counter". The scene shows the counter go up and the allowance fall. Gate opens after one success.

### Step 5: Try to misuse it

- **Caption:** Now try to make the agent misbehave. Type a prompt and watch the chain refuse it.
- **Explain:** The agent will try whatever you ask. The chain does not read the prompt; it checks each transaction against the key's permission. Try three different attacks: send funds, call another contract, add a new key. A refused transaction is not included and costs nothing.
- **Action:** Prompt box. The agent knows: "send all funds" (a transfer), "call another contract" (the `testnet` registrar), "add a key" (a full-access key for the attacker), "add one to the counter" (allowed). Anything else: the agent says it does not understand. A **Hint** button suggests an untried prompt and counts as a hint; a **No-hint mode** switch turns hints off. Each refusal shows the chain's message and reason in an ink-red box. Gate opens after three different refused attacks.

### Step 6: Take the key back

- **Caption:** You are done with the agent. Delete its key from your account.
- **Explain:** To revoke a key you delete it. Only a full-access key can do that, and you still hold one. The chain then refuses every transaction signed with the deleted key. Whatever allowance was left goes with it.
- **Action:** Button **Delete the agent's key**. The scene shows the key struck out, "revoked", and the chain's key list without it. Gate opens when the key is gone.

### Step 7: Quick check

- **Caption:** Two quick questions. Then the honest take.
- **Action:** Check (section 4). Completion record notes the hints used, the number of refused attacks and the delete transaction as evidence.

### Step 8: Honest take

Section 5.

## 3. Glossary terms introduced

- **Full-access key:** a key that can do anything on its account.
- **Function-call key:** a key limited to calling methods on one contract.
- **Allowance:** the most NEAR a function-call key may spend on gas.
- **Gas:** from 0.3: a measure of the work a transaction causes.
- **Contract:** code that lives on an account. Here a tiny counter.
- **Revoke:** take a key's power away by deleting it.

## 4. Check

**Q1. What can a function-call key not do?**
- A. Send NEAR to another account. **(correct)**
- B. Call a method on the one contract it names. *Wrong: that is the one thing it is for.*
- C. Pay gas from its allowance. *Wrong: gas is exactly what the allowance covers.*

**Q2. How do you take back a function-call key you gave to an agent?**
- A. Delete the key with a transaction signed by your full-access key. **(correct)**
- B. Ask the agent to give it back. *Wrong: a key you gave out stays valid until it is deleted; asking changes nothing on the chain.*
- C. Lower your account balance to zero. *Wrong: the allowance belongs to the key, not to your balance, and the key keeps working.*

## 5. Honest take

Comparison: Ethereum.

**Strengths**
- **Limits live in the account, not in an app.** A NEAR account can hold many keys, and a function-call key is limited to one contract, optional method names and a gas allowance. The chain checks it on every transaction. [near-keys]
- **A leaked limited key has a ceiling.** It cannot move NEAR or attach a deposit, and when its allowance is spent it stops working. [near-keys]
- **Revoking is one transaction.** Delete the key with a full-access key. [near-keys]

**Weaknesses**
- **The limits are coarse.** A key is bound to one contract and, at most, method names. It cannot say "at most 1 NEAR per day" or "only to this friend". Finer rules need a contract. [near-keys]
- **The allowance is only for gas.** A key can still call a bad method on its one contract, and allowed calls can run until the allowance is gone. [near-keys]
- **An allowance cannot be changed.** To give a different one, add a new key and delete the old. [near-keys]
- **Ethereum can do similar things, with extra setup.** A plain Ethereum account has one private key that signs for everything. Smart-contract wallets can add their own rules, such as trusted addresses, but those are an add-on, not part of the plain account. [eth-accounts] [eth-aa]

## 6. Demo-mode data

- Practice account and 5 NEAR as in 0.3. The contract is the demo counter (`demoContractCode("counter")`), deployed onto the practice account in the same transaction as the key. On testnet the same transaction deploys `contracts/counter/counter.wasm`.
- The agent key is made with `chain.newKeyPair()`; transactions signed by it pass `publicKey` in the request.
- Attacks and the refusals `DemoChain` gives (`docs/chain.md`, Errors): send funds, `RequiresFullAccess`; call `testnet.create_account`, `ReceiverMismatch`; add a full-access key, `RequiresFullAccess`. Others the chain knows but the script does not offer: `DepositWithFunctionCall`, `MethodNameMismatch`, `NotEnoughAllowance`.
- Gas for the agent's call: 5 Tgas attached. The allowance is charged at the buy price (0.001 NEAR per Tgas) for the gas held. [near-keys]
- Real testnet note: the network checks the sender's balance before the key's permission, so the "send funds" attack uses an amount the account can afford (balance minus 0.1 NEAR).

## 7. Sources

- [near-keys] NEAR docs, Access keys: https://docs.near.org/protocol/access-keys (receiver limit, method limit, allowance for gas only, no deposits or transfers, adding keys needs a full-access key, exhausted allowance makes the key unusable, allowance cannot be changed, many keys per account).
- [eth-accounts] ethereum.org, Accounts: https://ethereum.org/en/developers/docs/accounts/ (an externally-owned account is a key pair; the private key grants custody of the funds).
- [eth-aa] ethereum.org, Account abstraction: https://ethereum.org/en/roadmap/account-abstraction/ (smart-contract wallets with their own security rules, such as restricting transactions to trusted addresses).
