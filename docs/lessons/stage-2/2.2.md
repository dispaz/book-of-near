# Lesson 2.2: A call that takes several blocks

Issue: #18. Target time: 5 minutes. Chain access: testnet (practice account) or demo mode (DemoChain). Views: 2D and 3D, one shared state.

Topic: `2.2-topic.md` (the source of truth for what this lesson teaches).

## 1. Learning goals

1. Explain how cross-contract calls travel between blocks and shards as asynchronous receipts.
2. See how much gas each contract burnt, and that all of it is burned (contracts got 30% only up to protocol version 86).
3. Understand why independent receipts do not roll back automatically and how callback methods recover from failures.

## 2. Steps

### Step 1: Hook (When contracts talk to contracts)

- **Caption:** What happens when one contract calls another? On NEAR, they don't share memory—they send receipts.
- **Explain:** On chains like Ethereum, contracts call each other synchronously in a single instant. On NEAR, contracts behave like independent actors. When Contract A calls Contract B, the network writes a **receipt** that flies to Contract B in a new block.
- **Scene:** Two accounts (mailboxes) side by side: your account hosting Contract A (a standing scroll with a gear), and a Shop contract B (a scroll with a shop sign). No gate.

### Step 2: The receipt hop

- **Caption:** A call emits an Action Receipt. It executes in a later block, possibly on another shard.
- **Explain:** An **Action Receipt** is a delivery slip carrying your method call and gas budget. The target contract processes it in its own chunk. If it returns data, it emits a **Data Receipt** that flies back to your original contract's **callback**.
- **Scene:** Contract A's scroll produces a delivery slip (Action Receipt) that rises and hovers, addressed to Contract B's mailbox. No gate.

### Step 3: Trigger a cross-contract call

- **Caption (demo mode):** Order an item from the shop contract. Watch the receipts hop from block to block.
- **Caption (testnet mode):** Open a real call between two testnet contracts. Watch the receipts hop from block to block.
- **Explain (demo mode):** You attach 30 Tgas (Tgas: a trillion gas units; about 0.003 NEAR). Contract A reserves the order and sends the order slip. Contract B runs it in block #{firstBlock}. In block #{secondBlock} a data receipt brings the answer back to contract A's callback.
- **Explain (testnet mode):** This call was sent on testnet and recorded. Its sender attached 150 Tgas. Contract A, market, asks contract B, usdc, to send test tokens, and waits for the answer. Contract B runs it in block #{firstBlock}; the data receipt brings the answer back to market's callback in block #{secondBlock}. (Testnet mode shows the recorded call `HhRTH4KQ9n8WtsGv1VZ7S7UytSZHQxEdTCmyTCa38ULp`, spike 4, instead of sending one: a deliberate choice to keep the lesson free and fast. Its values were copied from the chain and match it; they are not re-read at run time.)
- **Action:** Button **Send cross-contract order** (testnet mode: **Open recorded call**). The delivery slip hops to Contract B in Block #{firstBlock}, executes, and hops back with a confirmation slip in Block #{secondBlock}. Gate opens when the callback completes.
- **Evidence:** `{ txHash, receiptHops: 2 }`.

### Step 4: Where did your gas go?

- **Caption:** The contracts burnt {gasSpent} Tgas of gas, and the NEAR paid for it was destroyed.
- **Explain:** Each receipt burns the gas its work used, and that gas is paid for in NEAR. The NEAR is destroyed: no account receives it, not even the contract whose code ran. Up to and including protocol version 86 (a version of NEAR's rules), the contract got 30% of it. From version 87, none.
- **Scene:** An ink gas meter appears, one part per contract, sized by the gas that contract's receipts burnt. The labels give each contract's exact gas from the call's own receipts; the bar is not to scale (a small part is widened so it stays visible). Every part is labelled as burned.
- **Action:** Button **Inspect gas**. Gate opens immediately.

### Step 5: What happens when a call fails?

- **Caption:** Unlike a single transaction batch, independent receipts do not roll back automatically.
- **Explain:** If Contract B runs out of stock or panics, Contract A's earlier state changes remain committed. Contract A's callback receives an error flag and must manually execute rollback logic to refund reserved funds.
- **Action:** Button **Simulate out-of-stock failure**. The delivery slip turns red with an ink cross at Contract B. The return slip signals the error to Contract A's callback, which safely unlocks the learner's reserved balance. Gate opens when recovery finishes.

## 3. Check

**Q1. If a contract you call panics and fails, what happens to your initial contract's changes?**
- A. They stay committed; your callback must inspect the failure and undo changes manually. **(correct)**
- B. The whole blockchain halts and rolls back the block. *Wrong: blocks are never rolled back for application errors.*
- C. Every change reverts automatically like on Ethereum. *Wrong: NEAR receipts commit independently across blocks.*

**Q2. What happens to the gas a contract's code burns?**
- A. All of it is burned. No account receives it. **(correct)**
- B. 30% goes to the contract's account. *Wrong: that was the rule up to protocol version 86. From version 87, all of it is burned.*
- C. It goes back to the user as a discount. *Wrong: burnt gas is paid for and destroyed.*

**Q3. Why are cross-contract calls asynchronous on NEAR?**
- A. Contracts can live on different shards and run in parallel without locking the whole network. **(correct)**
- B. Because WebAssembly cannot run mathematical calculations. *Wrong: WebAssembly is exceptionally fast at computation.*
- C. To force users to pay double gas fees. *Wrong: gas is priced accurately per computational instruction.*

## 4. Honest take

**Strengths:**
- **Reentrancy prevention:** Asynchronous receipt dispatch stops classic EVM reentrancy exploits (like the DAO hack) before they can start. [near-cross-contract]
- **Sharded scale:** Asynchronous calls allow contracts on different shards to interact without locking the global network state. [near-sharding]

**Weaknesses:**
- **No global atomic rollbacks:** Failure recovery requires defensive programming and custom callback logic to restore state and refund balances. [near-cross-contract]
- **Multi-block latency:** Every cross-contract hop adds at least one block (~1 second) to total execution time. [near-cross-contract]
- **Callback starvation:** If attached gas is consumed before reaching the callback, the callback never executes and state can get stuck. [near-gas]

Compared with: **Ethereum** (synchronous atomic calls, single call stack).
